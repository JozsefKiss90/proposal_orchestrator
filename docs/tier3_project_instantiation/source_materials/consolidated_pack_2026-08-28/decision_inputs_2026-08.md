1. Roland Hollós – Core modelling concept and scientific integration
Roland Hollós will have a central role in the scientific concept of the project, particularly in the integration of heterogeneous agricultural datasets and the development of the predictive modelling framework. His expertise in AI-based agricultural research, data integration, environmental and phenotypic data analysis, and sustainable agricultural decision-making is directly aligned with the overall objective of the project: to transform multi-source observations into reliable indicators of crop water stress and practical decision-support information.
His contribution will be particularly important for structuring the modelling workflow, integrating historical field observations with remote-sensing and physiological information, selecting appropriate AI/ML approaches, and interpreting model performance from an agricultural perspective. This expertise provides the link between the different data streams of the project and the final predictive framework for precision irrigation and crop-stress assessment.
2. Prof. András Jung – Remote sensing, hyperspectral harmonisation and data fusion
Prof. András Jung will provide key methodological expertise in remote sensing, hyperspectral imaging, field spectroscopy and multisensor data fusion. His role will be especially important for harmonising the historical hyperspectral measurements with current satellite observations and determining which spectral information can be reliably transferred or compared across different sensing scales.
He will provide scientific guidance on the integration of proximal, hyperspectral and satellite-derived information, including the selection of suitable spectral features and vegetation indices, spatial and spectral harmonisation, and the interpretation of differences between field-level and satellite observations. His expertise will therefore ensure that the historical dataset can be meaningfully connected with Sentinel-based monitoring and incorporated into the broader modelling framework.
3. Dr. Sándor Takács – Historical data, tomato experiments and primary validation
Dr. Sándor Takács will provide the primary experimental and agronomic basis of the project. He will contribute a five-year historical dataset that will be used to support model development and evaluation, providing an important temporal dimension that cannot be obtained from a single experimental season.
He will also contribute expertise in tomato cultivation, precision irrigation, crop water stress, experimental design and horticultural engineering. MATE will provide the tomato plant material, experimental trials, irrigation treatments, field measurements and agronomic information required for the primary validation of the project. His expertise will be essential for ensuring that the model is trained and evaluated against biologically and agronomically meaningful observations and that the detected stress patterns correspond to actual crop responses under controlled field conditions.
4. Prof. Gábor Milics – Precision agriculture and decision-support consultation
Prof. Gábor Milics will provide expert consultation in precision agriculture, agricultural digitalisation, GIS and data-driven farm management. His contribution will support the translation of the developed stress indicators and predictive outputs into a precision-agriculture context, particularly regarding spatial decision-making, irrigation prioritisation and practical applicability at farm level.
His expertise will help ensure that the final modelling framework is not limited to scientific prediction but can also be interpreted in terms of practical agricultural decision support.
5. Prof. Tibor Janda – Plant physiology and drought-stress validation
Prof. Tibor Janda will provide complementary expertise in plant physiology, drought stress and abiotic-stress responses. His role will focus on the physiological interpretation of the remotely sensed and model-derived stress indicators and on consultation regarding the selection and interpretation of suitable physiological validation parameters.
His expertise will strengthen the biological validation of the project by helping to confirm whether the stress patterns detected through remote sensing and modelling correspond to genuine physiological responses of the plants to water limitation.

AM I fitting and for what:My academic and professional trajectory provides a strong foundation for developing into an Agricultural Data Scientist specialised in plant stress, Earth observation and data-driven agricultural decision support. My profile has developed progressively from agronomy and plant protection, through plant physiology and environmental stress research, towards remote sensing, geoinformatics, precision agriculture, data analysis and digital decision-support tools. The MSCA fellowship therefore represents a continuation and consolidation of an already established interdisciplinary direction rather than a change of scientific field.
My initial academic training is firmly rooted in agriculture and crop science. I hold both a Bachelor’s and a Master’s degree in Plant Protection, which provided broad training in crop production, plant physiology, agrometeorology, soil science, agrochemistry, agricultural machinery, agricultural management, ecology, environmental protection, informatics, mathematics, plant breeding, vegetable production and related agricultural disciplines. This gave me a broad understanding of crop-production systems and the biological, environmental and management factors that influence plant performance.
I subsequently completed a PhD in Plant Physiology, where I specialised in crop responses to environmental stress. My doctoral work focused on the physiological response of maize to low-temperature stress and on approaches for improving stress tolerance. Importantly for my later development towards data science, my doctoral training also included courses in Statistical Software in Agrarian Sciences and Practices, Database and Statistical Software in Agricultural Sciences, and Experimental Methodology and Mathematical Processing of Collected Digital Information. These courses provided an early quantitative basis for designing experiments, organising datasets and statistically analysing biological responses.
This background was strengthened through approximately five years as an Assistant Professor in Plant Physiology and Plant Stress Physiology at the Agricultural University of Plovdiv. I developed and delivered courses, lectures, laboratory activities, assignments and examinations in Bulgarian and English, including teaching Erasmus students. I also supervised students and Bachelor’s research, participated in curriculum development, conducted research projects and contributed to field-based agricultural work and farmer consultation. My teaching experience included substantial annual teaching loads and international teaching activities, providing a strong basis for future knowledge transfer from the MSCA fellowship into university teaching and specialised training.
My research during this period concentrated on plant stress physiology, including photosynthetic responses, antioxidant defence, nutrient status and plant tolerance to abiotic stress. My publications include work on chilling stress in maize, photosynthetic performance, antioxidant responses and stress tolerance in crop plants. More recently, my research has moved increasingly towards drought stress, non-destructive physiological assessment and remote sensing, including work on drought-stressed tomato plants and UAV-based monitoring of crops under different irrigation regimes.
My current position at the Maritsa Vegetable Crops Research Institute represents an important step in this transition. My work includes non-destructive assessment of crop responses to environmental stress, development and analysis of multi-parametric datasets, evaluation of water-use efficiency, early detection of crop stress using remote-sensing technologies and collaboration with breeders to ensure that the research remains relevant to crop improvement and field application. This experience has moved my work beyond individual physiological measurements towards the integration of several information sources for crop monitoring.
In parallel, I deliberately expanded my profile into data analysis and computational work. During my work as a Data Analyst at KPMG Global Hungary, I gained experience working with large datasets and used Python, R, basic machine-learning techniques, Power BI and Excel for data processing, interpretation, dashboards and visualisation. This experience was outside my traditional plant-science environment and was particularly valuable because it strengthened my ability to approach data systematically and computationally.
My earlier work in agricultural market research also provided experience in collecting, structuring and analysing agricultural information. I worked with surveys and interviews involving farmers and agribusinesses, entered and maintained agricultural data in databases, ensured data integrity and used statistical software such as SPSS for analysis. This gave me an early understanding of the importance of reliable data collection, database organisation and the translation of data into information relevant to agricultural stakeholders.
A major step in my transition towards digital agriculture has been my international training and mobility. Through a COST Action PANGEOS Short-Term Scientific Mission at MATE, I gained practical experience in UAV-based multispectral and hyperspectral remote sensing for assessing crop physiological status under stress. My Erasmus+ activities further developed my experience with UAV data collection, hyperspectral imaging, field spectrometry and digital crop phenotyping.
My training at ELTE in geoinformatics and precision agriculture further strengthened this direction. During the mobility I applied geoinformatics tools in field experiments and gained practical experience in UAV data analysis and digital crop monitoring. This experience helped me understand how physiological crop responses observed in the field can be linked with spatial information and Earth-observation datasets.
I have also obtained a UAV Pilot Licence A1/A3, enabling me to participate directly in UAV-based data-acquisition activities rather than relying solely on externally collected imagery. My current technical skills include GIS, QGIS, Copernicus data access, UAV operation, basic Python, RStudio, data collection, data processing, data analysis and data visualisation.
My scientific networking has developed in parallel with these technical skills. I participate in COST Action PANGEOS, which focuses on Earth observation, precision agriculture and sustainable agroecosystems; AI4Insect, which applies artificial intelligence and digital technologies to agroecosystem monitoring; and REcrop, which focuses on resilient and climate-adaptive cropping systems. These networks have exposed me to interdisciplinary approaches combining biological science, remote sensing and data-driven methods.
My current research projects also demonstrate that this transition is already embedded in my scientific work. I participate in a project on the development of tomato lines and varieties resistant to drought and salinity, as well as a National Science Fund project on monitoring vegetable crops for precision agriculture using satellite and unmanned aerial systems, conducted in collaboration with the Space Research and Technology Institute of the Bulgarian Academy of Sciences. These activities directly connect my original expertise in plant stress physiology with remote sensing and digital agriculture.
At the same time, I am pursuing a Master’s degree in Environmental Engineering, which adds an engineering and environmental-management dimension to my existing biological and agronomic background. This is particularly relevant to my future work because irrigation efficiency and crop water stress cannot be considered only as physiological problems; they are also questions of resource management, environmental sustainability and agricultural engineering.
I have also already begun moving from scientific analysis towards digital implementation by initiating the development of an MVP for crop-stress monitoring and decision support. The purpose of this prototype is to bring together different types of information—including satellite observations, environmental data, field measurements and plant-physiological knowledge—and transform them into information that can ultimately support crop-stress assessment and irrigation decisions. Developing the MVP has made clear that my future role should not stop at measuring stress or producing remote-sensing maps; I want to understand and develop the complete pathway from biological signal to data, from data to prediction, and from prediction to an agricultural decision.
This is why I consider Agricultural Data Science the natural next stage of my career. I already possess a strong agricultural domain background that is often difficult to acquire through conventional data-science training alone. I understand plant physiology, crop stress, agronomy, field experimentation and biological validation. I also have an expanding technical foundation in remote sensing, hyperspectral and UAV data, GIS, Python, R and data analysis. What I now need to develop more deeply are the computational skills that connect these areas: multi-source data integration, machine learning, predictive modelling, feature engineering, uncertainty analysis, spatial and temporal validation and model interpretation.

Roland Hollós and HUN-REN will provide the central environment for strengthening my skills in AI-based agricultural modelling, heterogeneous data integration and predictive decision-support development.
