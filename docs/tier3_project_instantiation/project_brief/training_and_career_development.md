## Open Science, data governance and IP

FIELDWISE follows "as open as possible, as closed as necessary". At project start, HUN-REN
ATK, ELTE, MATE, Krumatic, AgroVIR and the fellow establish an inventory of relevant
background datasets, software, code, methods and other assets. MATE data are used for
approved scientific purposes according to institutional permissions; any use of
MATE-derived models or institutional assets in a future marketable product is subject to
separate legal and institutional assessment. Farmer and AgroVIR operational information is
minimised, coded/anonymised where appropriate and processed only for agreed
research/validation purposes. Pre-MSCA DrR background, FIELDWISE scientific foreground,
institutional datasets and Krumatic-developed code are separate rights layers, each
documented before the action starts (Pack D).

## Supervision, training and two-way transfer of knowledge

HUN-REN ATK is the recruiting organisation and primary scientific host. Dr. Roland Hollós
serves as primary supervisor and provides the central training environment for
heterogeneous agricultural-data integration, AI/ML-supported modelling, feature
engineering, validation design, uncertainty analysis, model interpretation and scientific
integration. His role as an AI researcher and HUN-REN AI Ambassador for the Centre for
Agricultural Research provides a strong link to the network-wide AI 4 Science environment.

ELTE participates as an associated partner, with Prof. András Jung as co-supervisor for
geoinformatics, field spectroscopy, hyperspectral/UAV methods, multisensor fusion and
scale-aware EO interpretation. ELTE provides the main spatial-sensing training environment
and access to the agreed UAV/hyperspectral infrastructure; Dr. Zsófia Varga supports
practical UAV operation, mission planning and imaging-sensor deployment.

MATE provides the longitudinal processing-tomato dataset and agronomic/irrigation expertise
through Dr. Sándor Takács. Prof. Gábor Milics provides senior consultation in precision
agriculture, GIS, agricultural digitalisation and translation of model outputs into
farm-level decision-support concepts. Prof. Tibor Janda at the HUN-REN Centre for
Agricultural Research provides complementary plant-physiology and abiotic-stress expertise,
ensuring that remotely sensed and model-derived signals are assessed against genuine plant
responses.

The two-way transfer is deliberately complementary. The fellow contributes agronomy, plant
protection, plant physiology, plant-stress biology, physiological phenotyping,
horticultural field experimentation, proximal spectral interpretation and the existing DrR
concept. The host and partners provide advanced agricultural data science, robust
modelling, uncertainty analysis, hyperspectral/UAV/EO integration, precision-agriculture
workflows and research-to-software/industry translation.

## Supervision cadence and governance

Weekly one-to-one meeting between the fellow and the supervisor. Technical sessions with
the co-supervisor through the EO-harmonisation strand, tapering to monthly outside it. A
monthly written progress record held by the fellow. A quarterly review against the Career
Development Plan, attended by both supervisors. During M25-M30, the monthly face-to-face
placement reviews (T5.3) join the cadence.

A Project Steering Group chaired by the supervisor (Dr. Hollós, ATK), with the fellow, the
co-supervisor (Prof. Jung — ELTE's named associated-partner seat) and one named contact
each from MATE (Dr. Takács), Krumatic (L. Krumov) and AgroVIR (Zs. Balázs). It meets at
M6, M14, M18, M24 and M27 — the milestone months plus the mid-placement review — and
issues a go/no-go verdict on milestones MS1 to MS5.

Decision rights: scientific method and day-to-day research choices rest with the fellow,
escalating to the supervisor; modelling methodology rests with the supervisor and the EO
strand's quality assurance with the co-supervisor, escalating to the Steering Group;
training plan, resources and milestone go/no-go recommendation rest with the supervisor,
escalating to the Steering Group; contract, finance, IP and open-access policy rest with
the host (ATK); access to partner data, instruments and field sites rests with the owning
partner, escalating to the Steering Group.

Conflict resolution has two steps: the fellow and supervisor resolve disagreements
directly and record the outcome in the monthly progress record; anything unresolved after
one cycle escalates within HUN-REN ATK by office, and then to the HUN-REN research
integrity route. Data- and instrument-access disputes follow the dispute clause of the
relevant access agreement. (The office-based escalation pattern is preserved from the
item 4 decision: routes name posts, not individuals.)

## Networking, communication and public engagement

Scientific dissemination targets at least two high-quality peer-reviewed submissions
during or shortly after the fellowship: (P1) physiologically grounded water-stress
signatures from the heterogeneous MATE archive; (P2) modelling, uncertainty and transfer
from historical experiments to prospective commercial-field validation. A third
methods/application paper on multi-scale UAV/Sentinel operationalisation and DrR may be
prepared if the independent dataset supports it.

Communication combines research audiences with farmer and student audiences: conference
presentations; MATE/HUN-REN/ELTE seminars; short teaching/knowledge-transfer sessions at
MATE and, where possible, AU Plovdiv; practitioner-oriented demonstrations of
stress/uncertainty outputs; an AgroVIR evaluation session; and, only if formally
established and suitable, a public-facing demonstration at the Szentendre model Bulgarian
garden linking traditional horticultural knowledge with modern environmental sensing.
Krumatic supports Bulgarian dissemination, demonstration and farmer-facing uptake of the
MVP. Operator-preserved measure: one public field demonstration with farmers and one
public-facing article per year.

## Career perspectives and employability

The researcher combines a BSc/MSc in Plant Protection/Agronomy, a PhD in Plant Physiology,
approximately five years of university teaching and research in plant stress physiology,
and an expanding technical profile in remote sensing, hyperspectral sensing, UAV
applications, GIS and agricultural data analysis. The fellowship addresses the remaining
competence gap — rigorous predictive modelling and model validation across time, sensing
scales and environments — completing the progression from plant physiologist/agronomist
using digital tools to an independent Agricultural Data Scientist specialised in plant
stress, Earth observation and decision-support systems.

FIELDWISE provides end-to-end experience from multi-year scientific evidence, through
leakage-safe modelling and independent commercial-field validation, to a web-accessible
MVP and industrial assessment. This profile is relevant to universities, European research
organisations, precision-agriculture and irrigation-technology companies, EO/geospatial
organisations, crop-phenotyping groups and FMIS providers.

## Non-academic placement: added value for the project and for the researcher

AgroVIR is the non-academic placement host: M1-M24 at HUN-REN ATK, M25-M30 on placement.
The placement supplies competences the academic host cannot provide alone: operational
model evaluation, research-to-MVP translation, agricultural-software requirements,
interoperability assessment, user-centred decision support and exploitation planning. It
is conducted mainly through remote collaboration with monthly face-to-face technical
meetings, subject to final company confirmation; it does not require unrestricted access
to proprietary customer databases or internal production systems — AgroVIR provides
protected, coded or anonymised extracts suitable for the agreed evaluation tasks. The
placement is supervised by Miklós Maróti, Managing Director of AgroVIR, subject to written
confirmation of the supervision arrangement.

By completion, the fellow will demonstrate that she has carried a scientific concept
through biological definition, model development, unseen-condition validation, software
implementation and industrial evaluation — a distinctive competence profile for both
academic and non-academic agricultural data-science careers. AgroVIR is additionally
consulted from M1 (T4.4), so the placement deepens an established collaboration rather
than opening a cold one.
