## 2. Impact

This section addresses the Impact criterion (evaluation weight: 30%) of the application to call HORIZON-MSCA-2026-PF-01-01 (MSCA Postdoctoral Fellowships 2026). It covers the career perspectives and employability of Dr. Rositsa Cholakova (sub-section 2.1); the dissemination, exploitation, and communication strategy (sub-section 2.2); and the scientific, societal, economic, and environmental impact (sub-section 2.3). Together the three sub-sections address all seven call-level expected impacts (EI-01 through EI-07) specified in HORIZON-MSCA-2026-PF-01-01 through project-specific mechanisms grounded in the five-work-package structure: Probabilistic multi-sensor diagnosis of the field state (WP1), Uncertainty-aware decision engine and demonstrator integration (WP2), Field validation, ground truth and uncertainty calibration (WP3), Training, supervision and two-way transfer of knowledge (WP4), and Dissemination, exploitation, communication and management (WP5).

### 2.1 Career perspectives and employability of the researcher

#### 2.1.1 The fellowship's career development rationale

Dr. Rositsa Cholakova enters the fellowship at approximately four years post-PhD, having completed her doctoral work summa cum laude in 2020 on the non-destructive evaluation of the physiological status of drought-stressed tomato plants (doi:10.2139/ssrn.5492527), with further peer-reviewed contributions on maize chilling-stress photosynthesis and biostimulants. Her research line is internally coherent: plant physiology, non-destructive evaluation of abiotic and biotic plant stress, and crop monitoring, centred at the Maritsa Vegetable Crops Research Institute, Bulgarian Agricultural Academy, Plovdiv, Bulgaria.

This expertise is the fellowship's principal justification from the fellow-to-host direction of the two-way knowledge transfer. Dr. Cholakova supplies the biological ground-truth engine — non-destructive physiological measurement of chlorophyll fluorescence, stomatal conductance, canopy and leaf temperature, and spectral reflectance (RQ4) — without which the field validation work of WP3 (Field validation, ground truth and uncertainty calibration) would be methodologically ungrounded. The Faculty of Informatics, Institute of Cartography and Geoinformatics geoinformatics group at Eötvös Loránd University (ELTE), Budapest cannot supply this expertise internally. Route B's covarying-patch design (RQ2) rests on a plant-physiological hypothesis — that a nearby homogeneous semi-natural patch covaries biologically with the target tomato crop — whose operationalisation and experimental proof (co-located seasonal campaign, RQ9) require the measurement toolkit and physiological interpretation that constitute the fellow's doctoral expertise.

The host-to-fellow direction is equally substantive. Eötvös Loránd University (ELTE), Budapest, Faculty of Informatics, Institute of Cartography and Geoinformatics provides geoinformatics and remote-sensing infrastructure, the hyperspectral imaging, field spectroscopy, and multisensor-drone fusion methods at the core of WP1, and the computational and Earth-observation framework within which the fellow's physiological insights are transformed into probabilistic, satellite-informed field-state estimates and irrigation decisions. Supervisor Dr. András Jung — Deputy Head of the Institute of Cartography and Geoinformatics, Head of the Multisensor-Drone-Fusion Working Group (project ELTE TKP2021-NVA-29), co-founder of Cubert GmbH and Brightic Research, habilitations in Computer Science and Crop Sciences — anchors this transfer. His dual habilitation signals that the disciplinary bridge this fellowship crosses has been independently traversed and is intellectually navigable.

The fellowship advances Dr. Rositsa Cholakova from postdoctoral researcher toward a cross-disciplinary profile at the intersection of plant physiology and non-destructive stress phenotyping with geoinformatics, probabilistic remote sensing, and uncertainty-aware crop modelling. This profile opens career paths in academic research groups specialising in remote sensing, crop science, or precision agriculture; in the agri-tech sector; and in research management roles requiring methodological breadth across environmental sensing and decision support. The project's confirmed TRL target — research prototype, TRL 3-4 (RQ10) — is proportionate to a 24-month European Fellowship: it delivers a research prototype and its foundational scientific evidence, not a commercial product. The mobility from Plovdiv, Bulgaria to ELTE, Budapest, Hungary (intra-EU) satisfies the MSCA-PF mobility requirement and constitutes a European Fellowship (EF, 24 months).

#### 2.1.2 Career Development Plan

The formal governance instrument for the fellow's career development is the Career Development Plan (D4-01, WP4: Training, supervision and two-way transfer of knowledge), prepared jointly by Dr. András Jung and Dr. Rositsa Cholakova and submitted as a project deliverable at the beginning of the action (month 3), in line with scope requirement SR-04 of HORIZON-MSCA-2026-PF-01-01. The Career Development Plan is reviewed and updated during the fellowship, providing auditable evidence of two-way knowledge transfer progression and training plan execution. It covers research objectives, the fellow's training and career needs across all mandatory transferable-skills dimensions, publication plans, and open science activities.

D4-01 is the operative instrument through which this fellowship realises EI-01 (improved employability and career prospects), EI-05 (alignment with the European Charter for Researchers via jointly prepared, periodically reviewed, transparent supervision arrangements), EI-07 (new mind-sets fostered through the formally documented interdisciplinary exchange), and EI-02 (enhanced networking and communication capacities as a named training goal). Specific quantitative career targets — publication counts, training hours, thesis co-supervision, follow-on proposal timing — are to be set jointly by Dr. András Jung and Dr. Rositsa Cholakova in the Career Development Plan at action start and tracked through its review cycle. These targets are the CDP's content, not the proposal's content; they depend on the fellow's evolving research outputs and the supervisor's mentorship capacity and cannot be fixed externally to the supervisory relationship.

#### 2.1.3 Structured training in the MSCA-PF mandatory dimensions

The fellowship's training programme integrates all key transferable skills and competences required by scope requirement SR-03 (HORIZON-MSCA-2026-PF-01-01):

**Digital and AI skills, including the responsible use of generative AI in remote-sensing and crop-water modelling and scientific coding.** The project's methodological core — probabilistic machine learning for multi-sensor Earth observation crop-water-state estimation — is the training environment. Dr. Cholakova develops competences in probabilistic inference, Bayesian calibration, uncertainty quantification, and model validation through WP1 and WP2 research tasks. The responsible use of generative AI is addressed in the disciplinary contexts where it matters for this project: remote-sensing analysis pipelines, crop-water modelling, and scientific coding, where uncritical automation risks producing calibration errors that would undermine the honest-uncertainty commitment.

**Knowledge valorisation, innovation and entrepreneurship.** The project translates the uncertainty-aware demonstrator (D2-01, WP2) toward user uptake, with associated partner AgroVIR providing the farmer-interface feedback channel. Dr. András Jung, as co-founder of Cubert GmbH and Brightic Research, provides mentorship in knowledge valorisation grounded in a demonstrated personal track record of translating research instrumentation from laboratory to commercial deployment.

**Research integrity and open science ethics, including FAIR data stewardship.** The project commits to open-access publication, FAIR release of all primary data and software, and an open validation protocol enabling independent replication. The Data Management Plan (D5-01, WP5: Dissemination, exploitation, communication and management), delivered by month 6, operationalises FAIR compliance across WP1 through WP3 data assets. Research integrity is embedded at the methodological level: the 90% prediction-interval coverage criterion (RQ5) is the project's adopted definition of honest uncertainty, ensuring that research integrity in the scientific-conduct sense is inseparable from the methods being learned.

#### 2.1.4 Two-way transfer of knowledge

The two-way knowledge transfer is the defining structural mechanism of this fellowship.

The fellow-to-host direction is substantive and non-substitutable: Dr. Cholakova's non-destructive stress-phenotyping expertise (chlorophyll fluorescence, stomatal conductance, canopy and leaf temperature, spectral reflectance, RQ4) is the ground-truth methodology for WP3 and the biological design logic behind Route B (RQ2). ELTE's geoinformatics group cannot supply this expertise internally. The host-to-fellow direction is equally substantive: Dr. Jung's group provides geoinformatics and remote-sensing infrastructure, hyperspectral and multisensor-drone fusion methods (Multisensor-Drone-Fusion Working Group, ELTE TKP2021-NVA-29), and the computational framework within which physiological observations are transformed into probabilistic field-state estimates and irrigation decisions. The exchange is formally structured in WP4 (Training, supervision and two-way transfer of knowledge) and documented in D4-01 (Career Development Plan).

The inter-sectoral dimension is provided by associated partner AgroVIR (Hungary) — a non-academic actor in the Hungarian agricultural sector whose involvement in WP3 field validation and D2-01 demonstrator feedback creates genuine research-to-practice boundary-crossing. AgroVIR provides farmer access and field-validation site logistics; it does not own or operate any project output.

This two-way transfer directly realises EI-07 (new mind-sets through international, inter-sectoral and interdisciplinary experience), EI-03 (stronger R&I capacity and knowledge transfer among participating organisations), and EI-02 (enhanced networking and communication capacities, through WP4 training in scientific communication).

#### 2.1.5 Contribution to EI-01

The jointly prepared Career Development Plan (D4-01, month 3) covering all mandatory training dimensions, genuine two-way knowledge transfer producing a cross-disciplinary profile in plant physiology and probabilistic geoinformatics, and the research portfolio from WP1-WP3 (outcomes OUT-1, OUT-2, OUT-3, OUT-4) together constitute the fellowship's contribution to EI-01: improved employability and career prospects of MSCA postdoctoral fellows within academia and beyond, contributing to a more mobile and highly skilled European research workforce.

### 2.2 Dissemination, exploitation and communication

#### 2.2.1 Governance: Data Management Plan and Dissemination and exploitation plan

Two deliverables under WP5 (Dissemination, exploitation, communication and management) establish the formal governance for all dissemination and exploitation activity, both due at month 6 — before any primary research deliverable is complete — ensuring open-access routes and FAIR compliance are confirmed in advance of the outputs they govern.

The Data Management Plan (D5-01, WP5: Dissemination, exploitation, communication and management), due month 6, defines the FAIR-principles compliance framework for all primary research data generated in WP1, WP2, and WP3, governing findability, accessibility, interoperability, and reusability for multi-sensor EO datasets, probabilistic model code, and the Hungarian tomato field validation dataset.

The Dissemination and exploitation plan (D5-02, WP5: Dissemination, exploitation, communication and management), due month 6, identifies open-access dissemination routes for all three work packages' primary outputs and establishes the timeline and channels for scientific communication and public engagement. D5-02 is the operative instrument for EI-04 (increased attractiveness, visibility, and reputation of Eötvös Loránd University (ELTE), Budapest and the ERA) through internationally positioned dissemination addressing an identified literature gap.

#### 2.2.2 Scientific dissemination: publications and open-source outputs

The primary scientific dissemination outputs are the peer-reviewed open-access publications arising from WP1-WP3, constituting project outcome OUT-5 (Peer-reviewed publications and open science outputs, WP5). Publications cover the probabilistic multi-sensor field-state diagnosis method (OUT-1, WP1), the uncertainty-aware AquaCrop decision engine (OUT-2, WP2), and the field-validation dataset and uncertainty-calibration evidence (OUT-3, WP3). All publications are made available under open-access routes in compliance with the Horizon Europe open-access mandate and D5-01, confirmed by D5-02 at month 6. Target audiences include the geoinformatics, remote sensing, and precision agriculture research communities positioned to evaluate the project's specific novelty: the end-to-end probabilistic coupling of multi-sensor Earth observation, crop-water-state estimation (root-zone soil moisture and crop water status, RQ3), and WUE-oriented irrigation decision support (RQ6).

The open-source release of project software constitutes a second durable dissemination channel. The probabilistic diagnosis code accompanying the Diagnostic model specification and calibration report (D1-01, month 14) is released as an open-source repository under FAIR principles (confirmed by D5-01). The Decision engine and demonstrator software (D2-01, month 20) is released as an open-source artefact providing the global precision agriculture and crop-modelling community with a reproducible, openly licensed implementation of the full end-to-end pipeline from Sentinel-1 SAR, Sentinel-2 optical, and PlanetScope (RQ8) observations through probabilistic crop-water-state diagnosis through uncertainty-aware AquaCrop calibration and counterfactual simulation to WUE-optimised irrigation recommendation (RQ6). The Field validation dataset and uncertainty calibration report (D3-01, month 24) is released under a dataset DOI or open repository reference under FAIR principles, providing an internationally reproducible benchmark from Hungarian tomato field sites for the probabilistic diagnosis framework and Route B covariation validation (RQ9).

#### 2.2.3 Communication to non-specialist audiences

Associated partner AgroVIR (Hungary) is the primary channel for communicating results to non-specialist audiences — Hungarian tomato farmers and the horticultural sector. AgroVIR's farmer access and field-validation logistics for WP3 create the operational agricultural context in which D2-01's decision-support outputs are presented to and critiqued by real agricultural users. This is an integral part of the project's scientific validation pathway: operational agricultural feedback directly informs the assessment of the demonstrator's usability at TRL 3-4 and tests the real-world credibility of WUE-optimised irrigation recommendations.

Curriculum integration at Eötvös Loránd University (ELTE), Budapest constitutes a second communication channel, reaching MSc and PhD students in geoinformatics, remote sensing, and precision agriculture programmes. D1-01 and D2-01, released as open-source code under D5-01, provide concrete teachable advances in Bayesian multi-sensor fusion and uncertainty-aware crop-water decision support integrable into ELTE's academic teaching — realising EI-06 (regular feedback of research results into teaching and education at participating organisations).

#### 2.2.4 Exploitation strategy and intellectual property

All primary research outputs — D1-01 (Diagnostic model specification and calibration report), D2-01 (Decision engine and demonstrator software), and D3-01 (Field validation dataset and uncertainty calibration report) — are research artefacts intended for open dissemination. No patent protection is sought. The project operates at TRL 3-4 (RQ10), and the primary value of its outputs is their contribution to the scientific literature and open-source knowledge base. The Decision engine and demonstrator software (D2-01) is the key exploitation vehicle: a replicable, openly licensed end-to-end decision-support prototype available to the global precision agriculture research community for further development and future TRL progression. Post-fellowship sustainability is secured through the open-infrastructure pathway: Eötvös Loránd University (ELTE), Budapest retains the validated methodology, code (D1-01, D2-01), and benchmark dataset (D3-01) as open research infrastructure within its Faculty of Informatics, Institute of Cartography and Geoinformatics, enabling continued use and extension beyond the fellowship period.

#### 2.2.5 Contribution to EI-02

Peer-reviewed open-access publications (OUT-5, WP5), FAIR data and software release (D1-01, D2-01, D3-01 confirmed by D5-01 and D5-02 at month 6), AgroVIR-mediated communication to the agricultural sector (WP3), and curriculum integration at ELTE — all embedded in WP5 and governed by mandatory deliverables — constitute the project's contribution to EI-02: increased and broadened research and innovation impact through enhanced networking and communication capacities with scientific peers and the general public. The fellow is trained in scientific communication and networking through WP4 (documented in D4-01), providing the communication competence to disseminate WP1-WP3 substantive outputs effectively.

### 2.3 Scientific, societal and economic impact

#### 2.3.1 Overview

The project generates impact through four complementary pathways, all calibrated to the confirmed TRL 3-4 research prototype target (RQ10): IMP-1 (Scientific advance in probabilistic multi-sensor crop-water-state estimation), IMP-2 (Economic and societal impact: water-use efficiency and farm resilience), IMP-3 (Environmental co-benefit through landscape-compatible sensing), and IMP-4 (Advancement of the researcher's career and the European Research Area, addressed primarily in sub-section 2.1). No claim asserts operational deployment, commercial uptake, scaled water savings, or quantified agricultural economic benefits that would be premature for a 24-month, single-researcher European Fellowship producing a research prototype.

#### 2.3.2 IMP-1: Scientific advance in probabilistic multi-sensor crop-water-state estimation

The project's primary scientific contribution is establishing a defensible end-to-end probabilistic framework in a specific, documented literature gap. The gap is stated precisely: across the reviewed studies, occurrence counts fall steeply as sensor integration deepens and as the task shifts from monitoring toward real-time decision support; the intersection of real-time decision support with PlanetScope high-resolution imagery is the single empty cell of the systematic literature review's research-gap matrix. This is a reviewable, specific, and narrow claim grounded in a review of 50 studies.

WP1 (Probabilistic multi-sensor diagnosis of the field state) develops and calibrates Route B — the homogeneous-patch proxy (RQ2) — fusing Sentinel-1 SAR, Sentinel-2 optical, secured PlanetScope imagery (RQ8), ERA5 meteorological reanalysis, and field observations into posterior estimates of root-zone soil moisture and crop water status (RQ3) for tomato (RQ1) at Hungarian field sites via AgroVIR. The Diagnostic model specification and calibration report (D1-01, month 14) is the primary scientific deliverable. Route A (direct probabilistic fusion) is also defined in the project architecture as a rigorous one-step alternative sharing the same downstream decision engine, maintaining the methodological breadth needed for a credible literature contribution.

WP2 (Uncertainty-aware decision engine and demonstrator integration) calibrates AquaCrop while retaining an honest parameter posterior, runs counterfactual irrigation simulations over weather ensembles, and recommends the option that best improves WUE (RQ6). The Decision engine and demonstrator software (D2-01, month 20) delivers the end-to-end coupling from multi-sensor observation through probabilistic crop-water-state estimation to uncertainty-aware irrigation decision recommendation — the integration the literature gap identifies as underdeveloped.

WP3 (Field validation, ground truth and uncertainty calibration) tests the framework against non-destructive physiological ground truth (chlorophyll fluorescence, stomatal conductance, canopy and leaf temperature, spectral reflectance, RQ4) and validates uncertainty calibration using the 90% prediction-interval coverage criterion (RQ5) — the project's adopted definition of honest uncertainty and pilot-success criterion (RQ7). The Field validation dataset and uncertainty calibration report (D3-01, month 24) constitutes this evidence together with the co-located seasonal campaign demonstrating statistical covariation between the proxy patch and the target tomato crop (RQ9).

The novelty argument is defended against the identified precedents — Bayesian Maximum Entropy drought fusion, copula-based drought-irrigation scheduling, dynamic Bayesian networks, MF-FusionNet — by the specific combination that each precedent lacks: all three of learned multimodal fusion, explicit probabilistic crop-water-state estimation, and WUE-oriented decision optimisation propagating uncertainty end-to-end. The precedents are respected, not dismissed. This scientific advance produces outcomes OUT-1 (Probabilistic multi-sensor field-state diagnosis method), OUT-2 (Uncertainty-aware AquaCrop decision engine), OUT-3 (Field-validation dataset and uncertainty-calibration evidence), and OUT-5 (Peer-reviewed publications and open science outputs), and directly contributes to EI-03 (stronger R&I capacity at ELTE), EI-04 (increased visibility of ELTE and the ERA), and EI-06 (research results feeding into ELTE teaching).

#### 2.3.3 IMP-2: Economic and societal impact: water-use efficiency and farm resilience

The project's confirmed decision objective is water-use efficiency (WUE, RQ6), chosen explicitly over yield maximisation or profit maximisation. The framework scores candidate irrigation decisions by WUE under the uncertainty propagated from WP1 diagnosis and the WP2 weather ensemble, recommending the option with the best expected WUE. The economic and societal relevance is direct: over-irrigation imposes direct economic costs (water, energy) and indirect environmental and social costs (runoff, soil degradation, shared water-resource pressure). An uncertainty-aware framework calibrating irrigation to actual crop-water status from multi-sensor Earth observation, rather than from generic scheduling tables, addresses over-irrigation at its methodological source.

The impact pathway is: WP1 (root-zone soil moisture and crop water status diagnosis) to WP2 (WUE-optimised counterfactual simulation) to D2-01 (Decision engine and demonstrator software) to outcomes OUT-2 (Uncertainty-aware AquaCrop decision engine) and OUT-4 (Standalone research demonstrator (web MVP)) to farmers and agri-tech stakeholders reached through AgroVIR. The pilot-success criterion (RQ7) — the 90% prediction-interval coverage target (RQ5) met on the validation set — is the project's verifiable threshold for whether the diagnostic and decision framework is credible enough to underpin its economic impact claim. Impact magnitudes are not asserted beyond TRL 3-4: the project establishes the probabilistic scientific basis for WUE-optimised irrigation recommendation for tomato horticulture, validated at Hungarian field sites, providing the foundation on which future higher-TRL development would build.

#### 2.3.4 IMP-3: Environmental co-benefit through landscape-compatible sensing

Route B (RQ2) sidesteps the sub-pixel resolution problem by selecting a nearby, homogeneous, semi-natural covarying patch that the coarse satellite pixel can resolve cleanly. This aligns the sensing methodology with landscape sustainability in a structurally significant way: the sensing strategy is positively correlated with the presence of natural or semi-natural patches in the agricultural landscape. A farmer who retains a semi-natural patch at the field margin gains a better-performing observation proxy; a landscape from which such patches are eliminated loses the observational infrastructure on which Route B depends. This co-benefit is a structural property of the methodology, not post-hoc framing.

The co-benefit is operationalised through WP3 via the co-located seasonal campaign testing the patch-covariation assumption (RQ9). The Field validation dataset and uncertainty calibration report (D3-01, month 24) and its embedded Route B covariation evidence document this result. The environmental co-benefit is stated as a structural upside of the confirmed methodology, not as a quantified environmental gain beyond the project's TRL 3-4 scope.

#### 2.3.5 IMP-4: ERA capacity and the host institution

Beyond Dr. Rositsa Cholakova's career contribution (sub-section 2.1), the project strengthens Eötvös Loránd University (ELTE), Budapest in two ways. First, WP1-WP3 produce genuine additions to ELTE's research output — D1-01 (Diagnostic model specification and calibration report), D2-01 (Decision engine and demonstrator software), D3-01 (Field validation dataset and uncertainty calibration report) — in remote sensing, probabilistic machine learning, and precision agriculture decision support, extending the Faculty of Informatics, Institute of Cartography and Geoinformatics research capacity into crop-water-state diagnostics and strengthening ELTE's R&I output (EI-03). Second, open publication and FAIR release of results addressing the identified literature gap increase ELTE's international citation footprint and reputation in multi-sensor EO and uncertainty-quantification communities, enhancing ELTE's attractiveness as a fellowship host and research partner (EI-04). The project's hosting at ELTE in Budapest, Hungary — a Horizon Europe widening country — situates this contribution within the ERA's objective of balanced scientific excellence across Member States.
