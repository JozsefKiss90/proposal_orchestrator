### 2.1 Credibility of the measures to enhance the career perspectives and employability of the researcher and contribution to his/her skills development

#### 2.1.1 Point of departure: Dr. Rositsa Cholakova's career position and the development logic of the fellowship

Dr. Rositsa Cholakova enters this European Fellowship approximately four years post-PhD, having completed her doctoral work summa cum laude in 2020 on the non-destructive evaluation of the physiological status of drought-stressed tomato plants (doi:10.2139/ssrn.5492527), with additional peer-reviewed contributions on maize chilling-stress photosynthesis and biostimulants. Her position at the Maritsa Vegetable Crops Research Institute, Bulgarian Agricultural Academy, Plovdiv, Bulgaria establishes her at the productive core of an agroecological research ecosystem in which plant physiology, non-destructive measurement, and crop stress monitoring are the primary scientific activities.

The fellowship is designed not to replicate this competence profile within a different host institution but to expand it into a substantially different disciplinary space: geoinformatics, probabilistic remote sensing, and uncertainty-aware crop-water decision-support modelling. This disciplinary expansion is the fellowship's career development logic. The mechanism through which it operates is a genuine bilateral capability exchange — not a hosting arrangement in which the fellow consumes training provided by the host, but a structured scientific partnership in which each party contributes capabilities the other cannot generate internally.

Dr. Cholakova's contribution to the exchange is the biological ground-truth engine that makes the project's validation methodology credible. The non-destructive physiological measurement of chlorophyll fluorescence, stomatal conductance, canopy and leaf temperature, and spectral reflectance (RQ4) is the scientific toolkit with which she collects the plant-physiological ground truth in WP3 (Field validation, ground truth and uncertainty calibration), against which the probabilistic multi-sensor diagnosis framework is calibrated. Without this expertise, Eötvös Loránd University (ELTE), Budapest's Faculty of Informatics, Institute of Cartography and Geoinformatics cannot conduct the WP3 validation protocol independently: the geoinformatics and remote-sensing group's competences are complementary to, not overlapping with, the fellow's plant-physiological measurement skills. This non-substitutability is the structural basis for the bilateral transfer claim and it makes the career development impact of the fellowship credible rather than nominal, resting on a real and verifiable disciplinary dependency.

Dr. Cholakova's doctoral thesis — on the non-destructive evaluation of drought-stressed tomato plants, the exact species (RQ1) and water-deficit stress type central to this project's field validation — means the transferred expertise is domain-specific and immediately productive for the host's research programme, not general and peripheral. The mutual dependency runs in both directions: just as the host requires the fellow's phenotyping competence, the fellow requires the host's geoinformatics infrastructure and probabilistic remote-sensing methods to execute WP1 (Probabilistic multi-sensor diagnosis of the field state) and WP2 (Uncertainty-aware decision engine and demonstrator integration).

The fellowship's intra-EU mobility — from Plovdiv, Bulgaria to ELTE, Budapest, Hungary — constitutes this action as a European Fellowship (EF, 24 months) meeting the MSCA mobility requirement (SR-01), and converts that mobility into career capital by embedding it within a scientific collaboration structured around a genuine capability gap on both sides.

#### 2.1.2 The Career Development Plan: governance, content, and accountability cycle

The primary formal governance instrument for the fellow's career development is the Career Development Plan (D4-01, WP4: Training, supervision and two-way transfer of knowledge), prepared jointly by Dr. András Jung and Dr. Rositsa Cholakova and submitted as a project deliverable at the beginning of the action (month 3), in accordance with scope requirement SR-04 of HORIZON-MSCA-2026-PF-01-01. This deliverable date — month 3 — is confirmed in the canonical reference pack and places the CDP submission before any primary research deliverable, ensuring that the supervision framework and training plan are established at action start rather than retrospectively.

The Career Development Plan operationalises the fellowship's career development commitments in four interlocking respects. First, it establishes the research objectives jointly — the supervisor's assessment of feasibility and scientific ambition is integrated with the fellow's research aims from the outset, not imposed after the fact. Second, it records the fellow's training and career needs across all mandatory transferable-skills dimensions specified by SR-03 — a structured, externally reviewed training plan that makes the development trajectory explicit and auditable. Third, it covers publication plans and open science activities, anchoring the fellow's scientific output commitments and her FAIR-compliance obligations (operationalised in the Data Management Plan, D5-01, WP5: Dissemination, exploitation, communication and management, month 6) within the CDP governance cycle. Fourth, and critically for credibility, the Career Development Plan is reviewed and updated during the fellowship — creating the periodic reassessment mechanism that keeps it a live supervisory instrument rather than a month-3 artefact filed and forgotten.

The joint authorship of the Career Development Plan by Dr. András Jung is substantively credible, not merely procedurally compliant. Dr. Jung holds habilitations in both Computer Science and Crop Sciences, serves as Deputy Head of the Institute of Cartography and Geoinformatics, and is Head of the Multisensor-Drone-Fusion Working Group (ELTE TKP2021-NVA-29). As co-founder of Cubert GmbH and Brightic Research, he has demonstrated direct personal experience of translating research instrumentation from laboratory to commercial deployment — the knowledge valorisation pathway that constitutes one of the fellowship's mandatory training dimensions. His habilitations in two disciplines that span the exact bridge this fellowship crosses — computational science and crop science — mean that his co-authorship of D4-01 provides the fellow with supervisory guidance from someone who has independently traversed the disciplinary boundary from biological crop science toward computational remote sensing and has a verifiable track record on both sides. This supervisor profile is not a procedural credential but a substantive career development resource.

The Career Development Plan is the operative instrument through which this fellowship realises EI-01 (improved employability and career prospects of MSCA postdoctoral fellows within academia and beyond, contributing to a more mobile and highly skilled European research workforce) and EI-05 (increased alignment of working conditions for researchers in accordance with the principles set out in the European Charter for Researchers), and contributes to EO-04 (increased alignment of working conditions in accordance with the Charter) and EO-05 (enhanced quality and sustainability of research training and supervision). Charter alignment is delivered through jointly prepared, periodically reviewed, transparent supervision arrangements in which the fellow's career needs are documented, reviewed, and held accountable by both parties under a contractually binding deliverable framework. No specific quantitative career targets — publication counts, training hours, thesis co-supervision arrangements — are fixed in this proposal. These are the Career Development Plan's substance, established by the supervisor-fellow partnership at action start in light of the actual research workload and evolving outputs; prescribing them externally in advance would undermine rather than support the charter-aligned supervision principle that D4-01 is designed to realise.

#### 2.1.3 Scientific and technical competence acquisition through WP1–WP3 research

The most durable career development measure in this fellowship is the acquisition of new, demonstrable scientific and technical competences through the research work itself. The competences acquired through WP1 (Probabilistic multi-sensor diagnosis of the field state), WP2 (Uncertainty-aware decision engine and demonstrator integration), and WP3 (Field validation, ground truth and uncertainty calibration) are the basis for publication, for independent grant applications, and for the cross-disciplinary career profile that the fellowship aims to produce. Because these competences are acquired by doing the research — not by attending courses about it — their acquisition is verifiable through the research outputs they generate: D1-01 (Diagnostic model specification and calibration report, month 14), D2-01 (Decision engine and demonstrator software, month 20), and D3-01 (Field validation dataset and uncertainty calibration report, month 24).

**WP1: Probabilistic multi-sensor diagnosis of the field state.** This work package requires Dr. Cholakova to develop proficiency in: harmonising heterogeneous Earth observation datasets including Sentinel-1 SAR, Sentinel-2 optical, and secured PlanetScope imagery (RQ8), together with ERA5 meteorological reanalysis; specifying and calibrating a probabilistic model that returns posterior estimates of the latent field state (root-zone soil moisture and crop water status, RQ3) as a full probability distribution rather than a point estimate; and implementing Route B — the homogeneous-patch proxy (RQ2) — within a methodological architecture that also defines Route A (direct probabilistic fusion) as a rigorous alternative. Working under Dr. Jung's direct supervision within the Multisensor-Drone-Fusion Working Group, Dr. Cholakova gains access to the group's hyperspectral imaging, field spectroscopy, and multisensor-drone fusion methodology and infrastructure — competences specific to this supervision context and not generally available through standard academic training programmes. The primary deliverable, the Diagnostic model specification and calibration report (D1-01, WP1), is a peer-reviewable scientific document that constitutes the public, citable evidence of this competence acquisition.

**WP2: Uncertainty-aware decision engine and demonstrator integration.** The prognostic decision-engine work requires competences in: uncertainty-aware calibration of the FAO AquaCrop crop model retaining an honest parameter posterior; counterfactual irrigation simulation over weather ensembles; water-use-efficiency-based decision recommendation under uncertainty (RQ6); and integration of the diagnostic and prognostic modules into a standalone research demonstrator. The specific combination of Bayesian crop-model calibration with weather-ensemble scenario evaluation and WUE-optimised recommendation is the technical competence that positions Dr. Cholakova to design and conduct uncertainty-aware agri-environmental decision-support research independently after the fellowship — across crop species and geographies beyond the tomato horticulture context of this project. The Decision engine and demonstrator software (D2-01, WP2, month 20) constitutes the software artefact evidencing this competence.

**WP3: Field validation, ground truth and uncertainty calibration.** The validation work package is the site where the fellow's existing plant-physiology competences are operationalised within a new scientific context. She leads the collection of non-destructive physiological ground truth (RQ4: chlorophyll fluorescence, stomatal conductance, canopy and leaf temperature, spectral reflectance) — the measurement domain where her doctoral expertise is directly load-bearing — while simultaneously acquiring competence in probabilistic validation methodology: prediction-interval coverage assessment (RQ5: 90% prediction intervals containing the truth approximately 90% of the time), pilot-success evaluation (RQ7: the RQ5 calibration target met on the validation set), and Route B covariation testing via a co-located seasonal campaign measuring the proxy patch and target tomato crop simultaneously (RQ9). WP3 is the work package in which the fellow most visibly operates as both knowledge provider (plant-physiological measurement) and knowledge acquirer (probabilistic calibration and covariation testing methodology), making it the most intensive site of genuine bilateral competence exchange in the project. The Field validation dataset and uncertainty calibration report (D3-01, WP3, month 24) is the archival validation artefact evidencing this work.

Taken together, the WP1–WP3 research outputs — D1-01, D2-01, and D3-01 — constitute a publicly available, FAIR-released portfolio of original scientific contributions that serve as the empirical evidence base for Dr. Cholakova's post-fellowship career applications across academic research, the non-academic agri-tech sector, and research management. These deliverables are not career development intentions; they are contractual deliverables with confirmed due months and confirmed content specifications.

#### 2.1.4 Structured transferable-skills training in the MSCA-PF mandatory dimensions

The fellowship's training programme integrates all key transferable skills and competences required by scope requirement SR-03 of HORIZON-MSCA-2026-PF-01-01, covering digital skills (including generative AI), Open Science, knowledge valorisation, innovation and entrepreneurship, and research integrity. The training is embedded in the project's own research activities and the host institution's research environment, ensuring that competence acquisition is contextualised within the domain of application rather than delivered as generic external modules:

**Digital and AI skills, including the responsible use of generative AI in remote-sensing and crop-water modelling and scientific coding.** The project's core methodology — probabilistic machine learning for multi-sensor Earth observation crop-water-state estimation and uncertainty-aware AquaCrop calibration — constitutes the digital and AI training environment. Dr. Cholakova acquires hands-on proficiency in probabilistic inference, Bayesian model calibration, uncertainty quantification, and computational model validation through the WP1 and WP2 research activities. The responsible use of generative AI is addressed in the specific disciplinary contexts where it carries methodological risk for this project: remote-sensing analysis pipelines, crop-water modelling, and scientific coding, where uncritical automation can introduce calibration artefacts that would undermine the honest-uncertainty commitment operationalised by RQ5. This domain-specific treatment of generative AI responsibility — integrating it into the technical methodology rather than as a standalone ethics module — provides training that is immediately applicable and is grounded in the concrete failure modes that matter for probabilistic Earth observation crop-water modelling.

**Knowledge valorisation, innovation and entrepreneurship.** Mentorship in knowledge valorisation is provided by Dr. András Jung, whose co-founding of Cubert GmbH and Brightic Research demonstrates a personal track record of translating research instrumentation from laboratory to commercial deployment. This is not entrepreneurship pedagogy but applied experiential mentorship from a supervisor with documented first-hand experience of the relevant translation path. The project's demonstrator-based approach — producing the Decision engine and demonstrator software (D2-01, WP2) as an open-source research artefact designed for user uptake — provides the practical context within which knowledge valorisation training operates. AgroVIR, the associated partner, provides the farmer-interface feedback channel through which demonstrator uptake is tested against real agricultural users, closing the loop between research output and practice-facing assessment.

**Research integrity and open science ethics, including FAIR data stewardship.** The fellowship's open-science posture is operationalised through the Data Management Plan (D5-01, WP5: Dissemination, exploitation, communication and management, month 6), which defines the FAIR-principles compliance framework for all primary research data generated in WP1–WP3 before those data are collected — a forward-looking governance commitment rather than a retrospective compliance audit. Research integrity is embedded at the methodological level through RQ5: the 90% prediction-interval coverage criterion is both the project's technical calibration standard and its definition of honest scientific reporting. A model that reports what it does not know with the same rigour as what it knows is simultaneously applying a technical methodology and implementing a research-conduct principle. The project treats these as structurally equivalent rather than as separate domains, providing the fellow with an integrated understanding of research integrity that arises from doing the research rather than from attending compliance training.

All three transferable-skills dimensions are directly grounded in the project's specific research content, the supervisor's specific professional background, and named deliverables that provide evidence of training completion and governance (D4-01 as the CDP recording the training plan; D5-01 as the FAIR governance commitment). This grounding is the basis for the claimed credibility of the training measures: they are embedded in the scientific activities the fellow will actually perform and cannot be decoupled from the research without the research itself collapsing.

#### 2.1.5 Two-way knowledge transfer, international mobility, and inter-sectoral exposure

The two-way transfer of knowledge operationalised through WP4 (Training, supervision and two-way transfer of knowledge) and documented in D4-01 (Career Development Plan, month 3) is the structural mechanism that distinguishes this fellowship as an MSCA career development action rather than a standard postdoctoral appointment.

**Fellow-to-host transfer (plant physiology to geoinformatics).** Dr. Cholakova's non-destructive stress-phenotyping expertise — chlorophyll fluorescence, stomatal conductance, canopy and leaf temperature, spectral reflectance (RQ4) — is the plant-physiological measurement layer that makes WP3 methodologically credible. Eötvös Loránd University (ELTE), Budapest's Faculty of Informatics, Institute of Cartography and Geoinformatics cannot provide this expertise from within its existing personnel. The transfer is therefore genuinely bilateral: the fellow brings into the host's geoinformatics research group a biological measurement capability that does not exist there, permanently expanding the group's capacity for plant-physiology-grounded validation of crop-water-state remote sensing beyond the duration of the fellowship. The permanence of this contribution — the validation methodology and the ground-truth dataset (D3-01) remain at ELTE as open research infrastructure — is the basis for the claim that this is a knowledge transfer, not merely a visiting appointment.

**Host-to-fellow transfer (geoinformatics to plant physiology).** Dr. Jung's Multisensor-Drone-Fusion Working Group (ELTE TKP2021-NVA-29) provides the geoinformatics and remote-sensing infrastructure, hyperspectral imaging and field spectroscopy methods, and the computational Earth observation framework within which the fellow's plant-physiological insights are transformed into probabilistic, satellite-informed field-state estimates and irrigation decision recommendations. The competence transfer is concrete and measurable: Dr. Cholakova's ability after the fellowship to design and execute a probabilistic multi-sensor fusion pipeline from Earth observation to field-state diagnosis (WP1) and to calibrate an uncertainty-aware crop model for WUE-optimised decision recommendation (WP2) are competences that exist at the host before the fellowship and are not currently in the fellow's profile. Their transfer is evidenced by the research outputs that could only have been produced through active engagement with those methods.

**International mobility between Bulgaria and Hungary.** The intra-EU move from Plovdiv, Bulgaria to Budapest, Hungary exposes the fellow to a different national research environment, institutional culture, research group organisation, and country-level science policy context within the European Research Area. This international experience is a career asset in both academic and non-academic hiring and contributes directly to the MSCA programme objective of producing a more mobile and highly skilled European research workforce. The fellowship's hosting at Eötvös Loránd University (ELTE), Budapest — in Hungary — additionally situates the ERA capacity contribution within the ERA's objective of balanced scientific excellence across Member States.

**Inter-sectoral exposure through AgroVIR.** AgroVIR (Hungary), as a non-academic associated partner operating in the Hungarian agricultural sector, provides the fellowship's inter-sectoral boundary crossing. The fellow's interaction with AgroVIR in the context of WP3 field validation — encountering agricultural sector knowledge requirements, farmer decision-making contexts, and the user-uptake challenges of moving from research prototype (TRL 3-4, RQ10) to practical farm decision support — constitutes an employment-relevant competence for career paths in the agri-tech sector and in roles requiring translation between research and agricultural practice. AgroVIR provides farmer access and field-validation site logistics; it does not own or operate any project output. This inter-sectoral exposure contributes to EI-07 (new mind-sets and approaches to R&I work fostered through international, inter-sectoral and interdisciplinary experience) and is formally structured within WP4 and documented in D4-01.

The two-way transfer further realises EI-03 (stronger R&I capacity and output among participating organisations and better transfer of knowledge between participating organisations): Eötvös Loránd University (ELTE), Budapest gains a permanent contribution to its research capacity in plant-physiology-grounded crop-water-state validation through the project's activities and open outputs, while the fellow carries the host's geoinformatics and remote-sensing competences into her subsequent research career.

#### 2.1.6 Contribution of the fellowship to the researcher's future career

The cross-disciplinary competence profile that the fellowship produces — a plant physiologist with demonstrated independent research capability in probabilistic multi-sensor Earth observation, uncertainty-aware crop-water modelling, and WUE-optimised decision support, evidenced by FAIR-released, peer-reviewable deliverables — is a specific and defensible career asset at the intersection of two active research communities (plant science and Earth observation) that individually generate large numbers of researchers but whose productive intersection remains underpopulated. This profile positions Dr. Rositsa Cholakova for career trajectories across multiple domains:

*Academic research.* Research groups in geoinformatics, remote sensing, crop science, and precision agriculture that pursue validated, uncertainty-quantified approaches to crop-water-state assessment require researchers who can bridge plant physiology and Earth observation. The fellow's demonstrated ability to produce a validated probabilistic framework — evidenced by the FAIR-released peer-reviewed outputs D1-01, D2-01, and D3-01 — provides an independently verifiable, citable record of technical capability. Open release of D1-01 (Diagnostic model specification and calibration report) and D2-01 (Decision engine and demonstrator software) as open-source research artefacts under D5-01's FAIR governance makes this record accessible to the international research community, supporting citation and collaboration opportunities that contribute to career visibility beyond ELTE.

*Non-academic and agri-tech sector.* The agri-tech sector and agricultural advisory services require researchers who can bridge raw Earth observation data and actionable farm decisions with quantified uncertainty. Dr. Cholakova's WP2 experience in the Uncertainty-aware AquaCrop decision engine (OUT-2: Uncertainty-aware AquaCrop decision engine) and her WP3 validation experience at real Hungarian tomato field sites through AgroVIR provide technically specific credentials for non-academic roles where demand for uncertainty-aware, physiologically grounded decision-support systems is growing but supply of researchers with demonstrated end-to-end implementation experience is limited.

*Research management and policy.* The successful execution of a 24-month, five-work-package MSCA-PF action — spanning WP1 (Probabilistic multi-sensor diagnosis of the field state), WP2 (Uncertainty-aware decision engine and demonstrator integration), WP3 (Field validation, ground truth and uncertainty calibration), WP4 (Training, supervision and two-way transfer of knowledge), and WP5 (Dissemination, exploitation, communication and management) — under a European Fellowship governance framework develops project management, cross-disciplinary communication, open-science governance, and research-administration competences that are valued in research management and science policy roles.

This three-pathway career profile constitutes the fellowship's realisation of IMP-4 (Advancement of the researcher's career and the European Research Area), delivered through OBJ-5 (Advancement of the fellow's competences and career) and measurable through outcome OUT-6 (Enhanced researcher competences and career readiness) produced by WP4.

The cumulative contribution to EI-01 (improved employability and career prospects of MSCA postdoctoral fellows within academia and beyond, contributing to a more mobile and highly skilled European research workforce) is: the jointly prepared Career Development Plan (D4-01, month 3) establishing transparent, charter-aligned supervision with periodic review; scientific and technical competence acquisition through WP1–WP3 evidenced in peer-reviewed FAIR-released deliverables; mandatory transferable-skills training in all SR-03 dimensions embedded in the project's own research context and supervisor's documented professional background; genuine two-way knowledge transfer producing a cross-disciplinary profile not reproducible by either the fellow or the host acting alone; inter-sectoral exposure through AgroVIR creating agri-tech sector employment relevance; and intra-EU international mobility advancing the ERA's skilled and mobile research workforce objective. Each measure is grounded in a specific confirmed project activity, a named deliverable with a confirmed due month, or a confirmed supervisor characteristic — not in aspirational statements about career development disconnected from the project's scientific and structural content.
