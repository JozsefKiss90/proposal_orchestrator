### 2.3 The magnitude and importance of the project's contribution to the expected scientific, societal and economic impacts

#### 2.3.1 Interpretive frame: calibrating the impact argument to the project's confirmed scale and ambition

The evaluation of impact magnitude must begin with a precise statement of the scale and ambition of the action being assessed. This project is a 24-month, single-researcher European Fellowship (EF, 24 months) targeting a research prototype at TRL 3-4 (RQ10). The impact architecture is calibrated to that scale. A research prototype at TRL 3-4 constitutes an experimental proof of concept validated in a laboratory or laboratory-equivalent field setting; it is not an operational system, a commercially deployed product, or a scaled agricultural intervention. The claims in this sub-section are therefore bounded by that calibration and do not assert quantified water-saving percentages, farm economic benefit estimates, or landscape biodiversity metrics that would require operational deployment, multi-season monitoring, or economic accounting beyond the project's scope and TRL. Any such assertion would violate the same epistemic standard — honest uncertainty propagation — that constitutes the project's core scientific contribution.

What the project does assert, and what constitutes its magnitude claim at TRL 3-4, is the following: the project establishes a defensible probabilistic scientific framework in a specific, independently reviewable literature gap, produces openly licensed and FAIR-compliant computational and data infrastructure that enables higher-TRL development by the global research community, and contributes a benchmark validation dataset from Hungarian tomato field sites via AgroVIR that is independently citable and reusable by the precision agriculture sensing community beyond this fellowship. One verifiable quantitative criterion is embedded in the project design: the 90% prediction-interval coverage criterion (RQ5) — 90% prediction intervals containing the true field state approximately 90% of the time on the validation set — which is the project's adopted definition of a well-calibrated probabilistic diagnosis and the verifiable methodological threshold for whether the diagnostic framework is credible enough to underpin water-use-efficiency-oriented irrigation recommendations (RQ6) at higher TRL. Meeting this criterion on the validation set constitutes the pilot-success criterion (RQ7). These two operator-confirmed quantitative criteria are the project's foundational claims of verifiable scientific quality; all impact magnitude claims build from them.

#### 2.3.2 Scientific impact (IMP-1): the nature, magnitude, and importance of the project's contribution to the state of the art

**The precise literature position the project addresses.**

The scientific impact claim is grounded in a specific, reviewable literature position: the empty cell at the intersection of real-time decision support and PlanetScope high-resolution imagery in a research gap matrix derived from a systematic review of 50 studies. Across the reviewed literature, the frequency of occurrence falls steeply along two gradients simultaneously: as sensor integration deepens from single-sensor toward multi-sensor fusion, and as the task shifts from detection and monitoring toward real-time decision support. The intersection of these gradients — the combination of real-time decision support with PlanetScope-level high-resolution Earth observation imagery — is the single underdeveloped cell of the reviewed gap matrix. This is the precise literature position at which this project acts.

The scientific importance of this location is not an artefact of review methodology. Multi-sensor data fusion for crop monitoring is methodologically mature; the combination of SAR and optical EO data for crop-water-stress assessment has been extensively developed. The frontier challenge is not sensor combination per se but the integration of sensor fusion with (a) explicit probabilistic uncertainty quantification — preserving and propagating the uncertainty inherent in the inference from observation to latent field state — and (b) an end-to-end coupling to a decision-support mechanism that uses that propagated uncertainty rather than discarding it at the decision interface. The gradient from monitoring to decision support is where the uncertainty challenge becomes operationally consequential. A monitoring dashboard that provides a point-estimate of soil moisture or crop water status discards the uncertainty in the observation-to-state inference; the user receives a number without knowing its reliability. A decision-support framework that propagates honest uncertainty from multi-sensor observation through crop-water-state diagnosis to irrigation recommendation provides the user with a quantity that is interpretable and actionable: the recommended irrigation option is credible at a stated probability level, not an overconfident single-point forecast whose reliability the user cannot assess.

PlanetScope's high spatial resolution — approximately 3 m — is precisely relevant to the decision-support use case: at the scale of an individual farm field, Sentinel-2 at 10 m may still integrate significant sub-pixel heterogeneity, whereas PlanetScope at 3 m can resolve individual field management zones. Secured access to PlanetScope imagery (RQ8) combined with an end-to-end probabilistic architecture is therefore not an arbitrary technical combination but a coherent target: the sensor provides the spatial resolution that decision support at the farm-field scale requires, and the probabilistic architecture provides the uncertainty-aware recommendation that the agricultural decision-maker needs to trust and use the output.

**The novelty claim: narrow, defended, and reviewable.**

The project's novelty claim is deliberately narrow. It does not claim that probabilistic approaches to crop-water-state monitoring are absent from the literature — four categories of genuine probabilistic precedent are explicitly acknowledged in the concept note and will be addressed in the project's peer-reviewed publications (OUT-5: Peer-reviewed publications and open science outputs): Bayesian Maximum Entropy drought fusion; copula-based drought-irrigation scheduling; dynamic Bayesian networks; and MF-FusionNet. The novelty claim is that the specific combination of all three of (a) learned multimodal fusion of heterogeneous EO sources at the PlanetScope spatial resolution, (b) explicit probabilistic crop-water-state estimation propagating honest uncertainty from observation to diagnosis, and (c) water-use-efficiency-oriented irrigation decision recommendation under that propagated uncertainty remains underdeveloped in the reviewed 50-study literature. Each individual component can be found in some form somewhere in the literature; their integration into a single end-to-end probabilistic pipeline is not documented in the reviewed studies. The claim is defended by the gap matrix evidence and by the explicit treatment of the four precedent categories — not by generic assertions about advancing the state of the art.

This narrow framing is itself a methodological virtue: an evaluator can assess the novelty claim directly by examining the gap matrix and the four cited precedent types, and the peer reviewers of the journals targeted for WP1 (Probabilistic multi-sensor diagnosis of the field state) and WP2 (Uncertainty-aware decision engine and demonstrator integration) results will constitute the scientific community best positioned to adjudicate whether the claim holds. The open-source release of the WP1 probabilistic diagnosis code accompanying D1-01 (Diagnostic model specification and calibration report, month 14) and D2-01 (Decision engine and demonstrator software, month 20) enables independent computational verification of the claimed architecture, strengthening the peer-reviewed publications beyond what closed-code studies provide.

**Route B as an architecturally significant methodological contribution.**

Route B (homogeneous-patch proxy, RQ2) is the project's primary diagnostic pathway: it sidesteps the sub-pixel resolution problem by selecting a nearby, homogeneous, semi-natural patch whose biology covaries with the target tomato crop (RQ1) and which the coarse satellite pixel resolves cleanly, with no heterogeneous signal to disaggregate. The significance of Route B beyond the immediate project scope is architectural: it is a resolution-agnostic sensing design applicable wherever a homogeneous covarying patch can be identified in the agricultural landscape. In European heterogeneous agricultural landscapes — where semi-natural patches, field margins, and fallows frequently adjoin cultivated fields — this design principle extends far beyond the specific tomato horticulture and Hungarian field-site context of the validation campaign. Route B's generalisation potential is bounded, not asserted as unlimited; the co-located seasonal campaign (RQ9) that demonstrates statistical covariation between the proxy patch and the target crop at the Hungarian field sites is the evidence required to assess whether the patch-covariation assumption holds under specific landscape and crop conditions, and its results are the data on which any claim of broader applicability must rest.

Route A (direct probabilistic fusion) is also defined in the project's architecture as a rigorous one-step alternative to Route B, sharing the same downstream decision engine. The existence of both routes within the project's methodological architecture ensures that the scientific contribution addresses the multi-sensor fusion problem at two levels of methodological sophistication, and that the comparative evidence — generated through the project's dual-route architecture — constitutes a contribution to the field's understanding of when proxy-based and direct-fusion approaches are each appropriate.

**The scientific contribution of the uncertainty chain.**

The project's uncertainty chain — the insistence that uncertainty travel honestly from multi-sensor observation through diagnostic inference to irrigation decision recommendation — is a methodological contribution independent of any specific sensor combination or crop application. Keeping the uncertainty chain intact requires (a) that the diagnostic model (WP1) return a full posterior distribution over the latent field state (root-zone soil moisture and crop water status, RQ3) rather than a point estimate, (b) that the AquaCrop calibration (WP2) retain an honest parameter posterior rather than collapsing to a single overconfident parameter set, and (c) that the counterfactual irrigation simulations over weather ensembles (WP2) score candidate decisions by their expected WUE (RQ6) under the joint uncertainty from the diagnostic posterior and the weather ensemble. Each of these requirements is a specific methodological commitment that can be independently verified in the open-source code and documented in the publications. The novelty of the end-to-end uncertainty chain is not the existence of uncertainty quantification at individual steps — that exists in several precedents — but the coupling of these steps into a single coherent pipeline in which the uncertainty from the earliest observation propagates, undiluted, all the way to the irrigation recommendation.

**Beyond the immediate scope: open research infrastructure as the primary scientific impact mechanism.**

The scientific impact of IMP-1 beyond the fellowship period and the tomato horticulture validation context operates through three complementary open-infrastructure mechanisms, all governed by D5-01 (Data Management Plan, month 6) and D5-02 (Dissemination and exploitation plan, month 6):

First, the FAIR-released open-source code accompanying D1-01 (Diagnostic model specification and calibration report, month 14) and the open-source D2-01 (Decision engine and demonstrator software, month 20) provide the global remote sensing and precision agriculture research communities with a reproducible end-to-end probabilistic pipeline — from Sentinel-1 SAR, Sentinel-2 optical, secured PlanetScope imagery (RQ8), and ERA5 meteorological reanalysis through probabilistic crop-water-state estimation (RQ3) through uncertainty-aware AquaCrop calibration and WUE-optimised recommendation (RQ6) — that can be inspected, replicated, adapted to other crops and geographies, and extended with additional sensors or decision objectives without the restriction of commercial licensing. The methodological architecture is not tomato-specific at the pipeline level: AquaCrop is applicable to a wide range of crops, and the diagnostic probabilistic architecture is crop-agnostic at the algorithm level. The scientific contribution extends to any research group working on end-to-end probabilistic multi-sensor crop-water-state diagnosis and WUE-oriented irrigation decision support globally.

Second, D3-01 (Field validation dataset and uncertainty calibration report, month 24) — released under FAIR principles with a dataset DOI or open repository reference — constitutes an independent scientific contribution as a benchmark dataset. A FAIR-released validation benchmark from Hungarian tomato field sites, comprising the multi-sensor EO input stack (Sentinel-1, Sentinel-2, PlanetScope), the non-destructive physiological ground-truth measurements (chlorophyll fluorescence, stomatal conductance, canopy and leaf temperature, spectral reflectance, RQ4) collected by Dr. Rositsa Cholakova under the field protocol of WP3 (Field validation, ground truth and uncertainty calibration), the uncertainty-calibration evidence from the 90% prediction-interval coverage assessment (RQ5), and the co-located seasonal campaign measurements from the Route B covariation test (RQ9), provides the precision agriculture sensing community with a resource for calibrating and comparing alternative probabilistic field-state estimation methods independently of the specific pipeline implemented in this project. Openly accessible benchmark datasets for probabilistic crop-water-state sensing are not widely available; D3-01's FAIR release addresses this gap in the open data infrastructure of the field and is a scientific contribution whose impact is not bounded by the project's own use case.

Third, the curriculum integration of the open-source code (D1-01, D2-01) and FAIR-released dataset (D3-01) into MSc and PhD programmes in geoinformatics, remote sensing, and precision agriculture at Eötvös Loránd University (ELTE), Budapest within the Faculty of Informatics, Institute of Cartography and Geoinformatics creates a self-sustaining research-to-education feedback cycle that reaches successive student cohorts without continued investment from the project team — the mechanism through which EI-06 (Regular feedback of research results into teaching and education at participating organisations, sustaining knowledge circulation between research and education) is realised at scale.

**Contributions to EI-03 and EI-04 through scientific impact.**

IMP-1 directly realises EI-03 (Stronger R&I capacity and output among participating organisations and better transfer of knowledge between participating organisations) by permanently expanding Eötvös Loránd University (ELTE), Budapest's research capacity into probabilistic multi-sensor crop-water-state diagnostics and WUE-oriented decision support — competences established within the Faculty of Informatics, Institute of Cartography and Geoinformatics through the WP1, WP2, and WP3 research activities, and retained there through the open infrastructure of D1-01, D2-01, and D3-01. The two-way knowledge transfer formally structured in WP4 (Training, supervision and two-way transfer of knowledge) — Dr. Rositsa Cholakova's plant-physiology and non-destructive stress-phenotyping expertise entering the host's geoinformatics group, and the group's hyperspectral imaging, field spectroscopy, and multisensor-drone fusion methods (Multisensor-Drone-Fusion Working Group, ELTE TKP2021-NVA-29) entering the fellow's practice — produces R&I capacity at Eötvös Loránd University (ELTE), Budapest that neither party could generate independently. IMP-1 realises EI-04 (Increased global attractiveness, visibility and reputation of the participating organisation(s) and, by extension, of the European Research Area) by placing Eötvös Loránd University (ELTE), Budapest at the source of the first publicly available, FAIR-compliant, peer-reviewed open-source implementation of an end-to-end probabilistic multi-sensor irrigation decision-support framework addressing the specific gap-matrix literature cell — increasing ELTE's citation footprint and international reputation in the multi-sensor EO and uncertainty-quantification research communities.

#### 2.3.3 Economic and technological impact (IMP-2): water-use efficiency and the pathway from validated prototype to decision support

**The economic logic of the WUE decision objective.**

The confirmed decision objective of this project is water-use efficiency (WUE, RQ6). This is a specific, operator-confirmed methodological choice that carries direct economic logic. WUE measures crop output per unit of water input: the WUE-optimal irrigation recommendation is the one that achieves the best expected crop outcome per unit of water applied, under the probabilistic uncertainty propagated from the WP1 multi-sensor diagnosis through the WP2 weather-ensemble counterfactual simulation. Choosing WUE over yield maximisation is economically meaningful: yield maximisation under diagnostic uncertainty can recommend defensive over-irrigation — accepting excess water costs to buffer against the risk of yield loss from under-irrigation — while WUE optimisation penalises the water cost of that defensive excess. In water-scarce or water-stressed agricultural contexts, this distinction has direct economic relevance: water input is not free, and its overuse imposes costs that are simultaneously economic (pumping energy, water charges or quotas) and environmental (runoff, shared aquifer drawdown, soil-structure degradation).

For tomato horticulture in Hungary — accessed through AgroVIR (Hungary) at Hungarian tomato field sites, in a continental climate with summer water-deficit pressure — WUE optimisation is both economically relevant and agronomically appropriate. Tomato is a high-water-demand crop for which irrigation management in both directions has direct consequences: under-irrigation causes water stress with measurable effects on fruit quality and yield; over-irrigation wastes water, can increase disease pressure, and reduces the efficiency of the water allocation on which the farm and neighbouring farms depend. A WUE-oriented decision framework calibrated to actual crop-water state (root-zone soil moisture and crop water status, RQ3) from multi-sensor EO data — rather than to a generic irrigation calendar or a simple soil-moisture threshold — addresses the decision problem at its appropriate level of complexity for the target crop and geography.

**The magnitude of economic and technological impact at TRL 3-4.**

The project does not assert quantified economic benefits — water savings percentages, irrigation cost reductions, yield impact estimates — that are not grounded in the project's actual outputs. Such figures require operational deployment, multi-season monitoring, and farm-level economic accounting at a scale well beyond a 24-month single-researcher European Fellowship producing a research prototype. The magnitude claim that is verifiable and proportionate at TRL 3-4 is the following:

The pilot-success criterion (RQ7) — the 90% prediction-interval coverage target (RQ5) met on the validation set — is the threshold at which the diagnostic framework's calibration is judged sufficient for its WUE-oriented recommendations to be scientifically credible. Meeting RQ7 means the framework reports what it does not know with the same rigour as what it knows: when it produces a 90% prediction interval for root-zone soil moisture or crop water status, that interval contains the true value approximately 90% of the time. A WUE recommendation built on a calibrated diagnosis — one the framework honestly characterises its confidence in — is qualitatively different from a recommendation built on a point estimate or an uncalibrated model. The difference is not initially quantified as a specific economic saving; it is stated as the scientific precondition for an economically credible recommendation at higher TRL. RQ7 is the verifiable, binary criterion for whether that precondition is met: the validation data (D3-01: Field validation dataset and uncertainty calibration report, month 24) provides the evidence record against which independent researchers can verify the claim.

From this validated prototype, the pathway to quantified economic benefit runs through higher-TRL development. D2-01 (Decision engine and demonstrator software, month 20) is the open-source artefact at which that pathway begins: it provides agri-tech developers, extension services, and follow-on research programmes with a reproducible, openly licensed end-to-end implementation that can be extended, operationalised, tested at scale, and economically evaluated in real agricultural deployment contexts. AgroVIR's farmer-facing assessment of D2-01's WUE recommendations during WP3 field validation constitutes the TRL 3-4 uptake signal: real agricultural users in the Hungarian tomato horticultural sector engaging with the demonstrator's outputs in their farming decision-making context. This is the appropriate evidence of economic relevance for a TRL 3-4 prototype — not quantified farm-level economic benefits, but documented engagement with agricultural practice that generates the practice-facing feedback required for credible higher-TRL development.

**The technological contribution: end-to-end architecture as a technology transfer foundation.**

The technological importance of D2-01 lies in its architectural completeness. The full pipeline — from multi-sensor EO data acquisition through probabilistic crop-water-state diagnosis (OUT-1: Probabilistic multi-sensor field-state diagnosis method) through uncertainty-aware AquaCrop calibration and counterfactual weather-ensemble simulation to WUE-optimised irrigation recommendation (OUT-2: Uncertainty-aware AquaCrop decision engine) — is implemented as an integrated, runnable, openly licensed software artefact. Its technological significance beyond the immediate scope is that it provides a foundation architecture from which higher-TRL development can proceed: the modular coupling of the diagnostic branch (WP1) with the prognostic branch (WP2) through a shared uncertainty representation allows the diagnostic module to be updated with new sensors, the prognostic module recalibrated for new crops, and the decision module adapted for alternative objective functions — drought risk, profit, market price — without rebuilding the architecture. This modularity is a deliberate feature of the project's two-route design (Route A and Route B feeding the same downstream decision engine), and it is the mechanism through which D2-01's technology transfer potential extends beyond tomato horticulture at Hungarian field sites to a broader range of precision irrigation applications.

#### 2.3.4 Societal impact: water sustainability, food security, and the European policy context

**Agricultural water use as a societal concern.**

Agriculture is the largest sectoral consumer of freshwater in Europe. Within agriculture, irrigated horticulture — including tomato production — is among the most water-intensive production systems. The societal relevance of precision irrigation decision support calibrated to actual field-state conditions rather than generic scheduling tables extends beyond the economic interests of individual farm operations to the collective management of shared water resources: aquifer drawdown, surface water allocation, and irrigation water quality are societal goods that individual farm-level irrigation management cumulatively affects. A research prototype demonstrating that multi-sensor EO data can provide the diagnostic foundation for WUE-oriented irrigation recommendations — with honest, calibrated uncertainty propagated to the recommendation level — contributes to the evidence base for the proposition that precision agriculture can reduce the water intensity of irrigated European horticulture without sacrificing crop quality or yield outcomes.

This contribution operates at the level of scientific evidence production appropriate to TRL 3-4, not at the level of operational water management impact. The fellowship cannot itself produce the large-scale, multi-season evidence required for policy-level quantification of water-intensity reductions. Its contribution is to establish the validated methodological foundation and open research infrastructure on which the research and policy communities can build that evidence through higher-TRL research and operational deployment. The open publication of methods, code, and data under FAIR principles (D5-01, month 6; D5-02, month 6) ensures that the scientific outputs are accessible to the European water research and agricultural policy communities without access barriers, maximising the societal diffusion of the project's foundational evidence.

**Honest uncertainty as a societal as well as scientific value.**

The project's methodological commitment to honest uncertainty — the 90% prediction-interval coverage criterion (RQ5) as the operational definition of calibrated diagnostic output — carries societal significance beyond its role as a technical calibration standard. In the context of agricultural decision support, overconfident model recommendations are not merely scientifically incorrect; they carry real economic risk for farmers who act on a recommendation presented as more reliable than its evidence warrants. An uncertainty-aware decision framework that explicitly characterises the reliability of its recommendations — producing, for each candidate irrigation decision, a WUE score and an associated uncertainty range rather than a deterministic recommendation — is a form of technological transparency in agricultural AI that respects the farmer's capacity to incorporate uncertainty into their own decision-making context. This dimension of the project's uncertainty architecture is a design choice with societal relevance independent of the specific crop or geography: it demonstrates that research-prototype precision agriculture decision support can be built on the principle of honest uncertainty rather than on false precision, contributing to the broader social goal of trustworthy and interpretable agricultural technology.

**Contribution to EI-01 and EI-07 through societal impact.**

The societal impact of IMP-2 contributes to EI-01 (Improved employability and career prospects of MSCA postdoctoral fellows within academia and beyond, contributing to a more mobile and highly skilled European research workforce) through the specific career-relevant competence it delivers: Dr. Rositsa Cholakova's demonstrated ability to design, implement, validate, and communicate an uncertainty-aware crop-water-state decision-support prototype creates a specific and defensible employment profile at the intersection of plant physiology and probabilistic geoinformatics — a profile directly relevant to agri-tech sector hiring, academic positions in precision agriculture or remote sensing research groups, and research management roles requiring evidence-based translation between Earth observation data and agricultural decision-making. The societal impact of IMP-2 contributes to EI-07 (New mind-sets and approaches to R&I work fostered through international, inter-sectoral and interdisciplinary experience, enriching the broader European research culture) through AgroVIR's inter-sectoral engagement in WP3 (Field validation, ground truth and uncertainty calibration): the encounter between the research team's probabilistic EO methodology and the agricultural sector's practical decision-making context — mediated by AgroVIR's farmer access and field-validation logistics — generates the cross-sectoral insight that WUE-oriented recommendations must be presented in forms legible to non-specialist agricultural users, directly informing the demonstrator's user-interface design (OUT-4: Standalone research demonstrator (web MVP)) and the project's assessment of its own TRL 3-4 operational credibility.

#### 2.3.5 Environmental co-benefit through landscape-compatible sensing (IMP-3)

**The structural environmental logic of Route B.**

Route B (homogeneous-patch proxy, RQ2) creates an environmental co-benefit that is inseparable from its core sensing design. The Route B strategy requires the identification of a nearby, homogeneous, semi-natural patch that covaries with the target tomato crop (RQ1) and that the coarse satellite pixel resolves cleanly. The sensing performance of Route B is therefore positively correlated with the presence of natural and semi-natural patches in the agricultural landscape: a farmer who retains a semi-natural patch at the field margin gains a better-performing observation proxy; a landscape from which such patches have been eliminated loses the observational infrastructure on which Route B depends. This alignment between sensing performance and landscape heterogeneity is a structural property of the methodology — not a post-hoc sustainability framing appended to the design.

The environmental co-benefit argument is not that Route B incentivises biodiversity conservation through an explicit economic mechanism; no such mechanism is claimed or proposed. The argument is narrower and methodologically grounded: a precision irrigation sensing framework whose performance improves with the presence of semi-natural landscape elements provides a positive signal about the value of landscape diversity from within the agricultural technology system itself. In the context of European agricultural policy — where landscape diversity, habitat retention, and sustainable land management are recognised priorities — a methodology that rewards rather than ignores the presence of semi-natural field-margin patches is structurally aligned with landscape sustainability objectives in a way that pure high-resolution satellite approaches are not.

**Validation of the co-benefit through RQ9 and D3-01.**

The Route B patch-covariation assumption is not asserted but operationally tested. The co-located seasonal campaign (RQ9) measures both the proxy patch and the target tomato crop simultaneously throughout the growing season, demonstrating statistical covariation between the two at the level of soil moisture and crop water status. Meeting the RQ9 standard on the validation set is the verifiable indicator of the environmental co-benefit: it constitutes empirical evidence that the landscape-compatible sensing design is not merely methodologically proposed but validated under real agricultural landscape conditions at the Hungarian field sites accessed via AgroVIR. D3-01 (Field validation dataset and uncertainty calibration report, month 24) documents this covariation evidence as a FAIR-released open dataset — available to other research groups to assess whether the patch-covariation principle holds in other landscape and crop contexts, extending the environmental co-benefit validation beyond the project's own scope.

The magnitude of IMP-3 at TRL 3-4 is the validation of the methodological principle under specific field conditions. Quantification of landscape biodiversity outcomes or soil-health metrics would require a scale of monitoring and controlled comparison well beyond a 24-month fellowship. The project's contribution is to demonstrate the principle, validate the assumption, and release the evidence openly — the scientific precondition for any future assessment of Route B's environmental co-benefit at landscape scale.

#### 2.3.6 Quantification of impact: what is verifiable at TRL 3-4 and why the absence of further quantification is principled

A proposal for an evaluator-oriented assessment of impact magnitude is expected to provide quantified estimates where possible and meaningful. This sub-section provides those estimates precisely where they are grounded in the project's confirmed methods and outputs, and explicitly declines to assert quantification where premature for a TRL 3-4 prototype. The absence of non-grounded quantification is not a rhetorical gap; it is the application of the same epistemic discipline — honest uncertainty about what the evidence warrants — that is the scientific core of the project.

**What is quantified and verifiable:**

The 90% prediction-interval coverage criterion (RQ5) is the project's primary quantitative impact claim. It is an independently checkable, binary-assessed standard borrowed from established statistical calibration practice — analogous standards are deployed in probabilistic weather forecasting and financial risk modelling — and its application to crop-water-state diagnosis under multi-sensor EO data constitutes the project's adoption of an established quantitative quality standard at a new methodological junction. The criterion is assessed on the validation set collected in WP3 (Field validation, ground truth and uncertainty calibration): either the 90% prediction intervals contain the true values approximately 90% of the time or they do not. Any independent researcher with access to D3-01 (Field validation dataset and uncertainty calibration report, month 24) and the D1-01 (Diagnostic model specification and calibration report, month 14) open-source code can reproduce the calibration assessment. This standard of verifiability — independently reproducible from open data and open code — represents the highest level of scientific accountability available at TRL 3-4.

The pilot-success criterion (RQ7) — the RQ5 calibration target met on the validation set — is the project's binary outcome criterion. Its verification is a direct function of D3-01's validation data and the RQ5 calibration assessment. This criterion provides the evaluator with a single, clear, verifiable success standard for the project's scientific core.

The 50-study systematic literature review underpins the gap matrix from which the scientific novelty claim is derived. The number of reviewed studies is an independently verifiable fact of the literature review. The emptiness of the real-time decision support combined with PlanetScope cell of the gap matrix is an assessable empirical claim about the reviewed literature, verifiable by an independent literature reviewer or peer reviewer.

TRL 3-4 (RQ10) is a confirmed, quantified maturity target. TRL 3-4 specifies: experimental proof of concept through to technology validated in laboratory or simulated operational environment. This is the appropriate, proportionate ambition for a 24-month single-researcher fellowship, and it is the framework within which all impact magnitude claims must be assessed by evaluators.

**What is not quantified and why:**

The project does not assert specific water-saving percentages, irrigation cost reductions, agricultural economic benefit estimates, biodiversity impact metrics, or carbon footprint reductions. These would require operational deployment, multi-season monitoring, and evidence-collection capacity well beyond the project's scope. Asserting such quantities without the evidence base would violate the methodological commitment to honest uncertainty that is the scientific core of this project. A project whose central scientific contribution is the 90% prediction-interval coverage criterion — the principled refusal to claim more certainty than the evidence warrants — cannot appropriately assert economic impact magnitudes that the evidence does not support. The evaluator is invited to treat the absence of fabricated impact metrics as a signal of scientific integrity rather than of insufficient ambition.

The following mapping provides the 2.3-perspective contribution — through scientific, societal, and economic impact mechanisms — to each of the seven expected impacts (EI-01 through EI-07) specified for HORIZON-MSCA-2026-PF-01-01, complementing the career (2.1) and dissemination (2.2) perspectives.

**EI-01: Improved employability and career prospects of MSCA postdoctoral fellows within academia and beyond, contributing to a more mobile and highly skilled European research workforce.**

The scientific output portfolio of WP1 through WP3 — OUT-1 (Probabilistic multi-sensor field-state diagnosis method), OUT-2 (Uncertainty-aware AquaCrop decision engine), OUT-3 (Field-validation dataset and uncertainty-calibration evidence) — constitutes the empirical and technological evidence base for Dr. Rositsa Cholakova's post-fellowship career across academic research, the agri-tech sector, and research management. **EI-02: Increased and broadened research and innovation impact through enhanced networking and communication capacities with scientific peers and the general public.**

The project's scientific dissemination of peer-reviewed open-access publications (OUT-5) addressing a specific, reviewable literature gap creates the conditions for citation-driven networking and scientific community engagement with the remote sensing, precision agriculture, and crop physiology communities addressed by WP1, WP2, and WP3 respectively. The two-way knowledge transfer formally structured in WP4 (Training, supervision and two-way transfer of knowledge) and documented in D4-01 (Career Development Plan, month 3) — Dr. Rositsa Cholakova's plant-physiology expertise into the geoinformatics group, and Dr. András Jung's group's geoinformatics and hyperspectral-fusion methods into the fellow's practice — constitutes the knowledge transfer between participating organisations that EI-03 specifies, producing R&I capacity that neither party could generate independently.

Open-access publication of results addressing a specific, reviewable gap in probabilistic multi-sensor EO irrigation decision support, governed by D5-02 (Dissemination and exploitation plan, month 6), and the FAIR release of D3-01 as an internationally citable benchmark dataset from Hungarian tomato field sites, increase Eötvös Loránd University (ELTE), Budapest's citation footprint and reputation in the multi-sensor EO and uncertainty-quantification research communities.
