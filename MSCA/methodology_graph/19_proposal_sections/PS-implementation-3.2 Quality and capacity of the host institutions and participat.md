3.2  QUALITY AND CAPACITY OF THE HOST INSTITUTIONS AND PARTICIPATING ORGANISATIONS, INCLUDING HOSTING ARRANGEMENTS

For a European Fellowship of the single-researcher, single-beneficiary MSCA-PF type, section 3.2 addresses three distinct evaluative questions. First: does the sole beneficiary have the institutional standing, infrastructure, scientific environment, and supervisory capacity to host a postdoctoral fellow and enable genuine research progress? Second: are the hosting arrangements concrete, specific, and structured so as to support the fellow's integration into the team and the two-way exchange of knowledge that the instrument demands? Third: do any associated partners linked to the beneficiary have a real, scoped, and proportionate role in the project? Each question is answered below with reference to confirmed project facts. Full organisational profiles are provided in Part B-2 Section 5.

This is a European Fellowship; there is no outgoing Third Country host and no return phase, so no Global Fellowship outgoing or return host description is required or applicable.

The host institution: Eötvös Loránd University (ELTE), Budapest

The sole beneficiary and host institution is Eötvös Loránd University (ELTE), Budapest, Hungary. The specific hosting unit is the Faculty of Informatics, Institute of Cartography and Geoinformatics, whose group focus is geoinformatics and remote sensing. ELTE is a higher education establishment in an EU Member State (Hungary, country code HU), satisfying the legal-entity eligibility condition for MSCA-PF hosting. As a higher education establishment in an EU Member State, ELTE is required to have a gender equality plan in place at grant signature and throughout the fellowship — a mandatory condition under the applicable programme provisions.

The Institute of Cartography and Geoinformatics is the institutional home for the fellowship and provides the scientific infrastructure on which the project's Earth-observation and geospatial methodology depends. The group's competence in geoinformatics and remote sensing is not a peripheral capability engaged only at the margins of the project: it is the methodological axis of WP1 (Probabilistic multi-sensor diagnosis of the field state) and WP2 (Uncertainty-aware decision engine and demonstrator integration). The multi-sensor Earth-observation data integration tasks in T1-01 and T1-02, and the probabilistic modelling and software integration tasks in T2-01 through T2-03, are all executed within a disciplinary context that the host group inhabits as a matter of normal scientific practice. The fellow is not being placed in a tangentially related environment that offers generic computing facilities while her methods live elsewhere; she is being placed in the precise disciplinary location where the methods she is acquiring through the fellowship are researched, developed, and applied.

The host provides the following concrete infrastructure for the project:

Geospatial and remote-sensing computing infrastructure: platforms capable of ingesting, quality-controlling, and harmonising the full multi-sensor data stack — Sentinel-1 SAR (10–20 m resolution), Sentinel-2 optical (10 m), PlanetScope high-resolution imagery, and ERA5 meteorological reanalysis — at the spatial and temporal scales required by WP1's T1-01 (Acquire and harmonise multi-sensor EO and meteorological data, months 1–6). This infrastructure is required from the first month of the fellowship, when T1-01 begins data ingestion, and remains in continuous use throughout the 14-month span of WP1 and the duration of WP2 and WP3.

Hyperspectral and drone-borne field instrumentation: including the equipment and methodological infrastructure of the Multisensor-Drone-Fusion Working Group (project ELTE TKP2021-NVA-29), which Dr. András Jung leads as Head. The Working Group's drone-borne multisensor-fusion capabilities are directly relevant to the EO data integration work in T1-01 and T1-02, and the hyperspectral field spectroscopy instruments that the group operates are complementary to the spectral reflectance ground-truth measurements specified in the validation protocol (RQ4, T3-01). The Working Group is not an external collaboration but an internal institutional resource embedded in the same institute.

Active engagement with the Hungarian geospatial and agricultural research landscape: the Institute's relationships within the Hungarian research community provide the institutional context in which the fellow's integration, dissemination, and networking activities take place. The WP5 (Dissemination, exploitation, communication and management) communication strategy targets, among others, the host's geoinformatics and remote-sensing community, and this target audience is immediately accessible through the host institution's normal academic activities.

The specificity and depth of the host's infrastructure match is what distinguishes this fellowship from a generic hosting arrangement. The evaluator can observe that the host group does not need to be briefed on what a Sentinel-1 SAR time series is, does not need to learn what hyperspectral field spectroscopy involves, and does not need to construct probabilistic modelling computing environments from scratch: these are pre-existing capabilities into which the fellow's project is inserted, substantially reducing the execution risk associated with infrastructure establishment.

The supervisor: Dr. András Jung

The confirmed supervisor is Dr. András Jung, holding the position of Deputy Head, Institute of Cartography and Geoinformatics, ELTE Faculty of Informatics. His expertise spans remote sensing, hyperspectral imaging and field spectroscopy, and multisensor-drone fusion. He is Head of the Multisensor-Drone-Fusion Working Group (project ELTE TKP2021-NVA-29).

Dr. András Jung's supervisory credentials extend in two directions that are both material to this project. On the research and infrastructure side, his remote-sensing and multisensor-fusion expertise is the methodological backbone of WP1 and WP2: he contributes scientific expertise in remote sensing, hyperspectral imaging, field spectroscopy, and multisensor-drone fusion to T1-01 (data acquisition and harmonisation) and T1-02 (Route A and Route B implementation) in WP1, and to T2-01 (uncertainty-aware AquaCrop calibration) in WP2, as confirmed by the WP structure and task assignment. These are not nominal contributions; the supervisory input in T1-01 and T1-02 is the mechanism through which the host's Earth-observation methods are transferred to the fellow in practice.

On the knowledge-valorisation and entrepreneurship side, Dr. András Jung is co-founder of Cubert GmbH (hyperspectral cameras) and Brightic Research, demonstrating a documented history of translating hyperspectral and remote-sensing research into instrumentation and applied contexts. This is directly relevant to the knowledge-valorisation and innovation-and-entrepreneurship training dimension structured in WP4 (Training, supervision and two-way transfer of knowledge): the supervisor has personally navigated the pathway from research to commercialisation in the hyperspectral imaging domain, and can mentor the fellow on the corresponding translation pathway for the uncertainty-aware irrigation decision-support demonstrator developed in WP2 and WP5. The training in this dimension is thus grounded in the supervisor's own professional experience, not in generic course content.

Critically, Dr. András Jung holds habilitations in both Computer Science and Crop Sciences. This dual habilitation is substantively significant for this project, which sits at the boundary between probabilistic machine learning and Earth observation on one side, and crop physiology and agronomy on the other. A supervisor with habilitation only in Computer Science could guide the EO methodology but would lack the agronomic frame within which the project's outputs — irrigation recommendations, water-use efficiency assessment, crop-water-status estimation — are scientifically meaningful. A supervisor with habilitation only in Crop Sciences could interpret the agronomic outputs but could not guide the probabilistic machine-learning and EO integration methodology that WP1 and WP2 require. The dual habilitation closes this gap: it provides a supervisor who is academically credentialled in both the computational and the crop-science domains, appropriate for a fellow who is a plant physiologist (Dr. Rositsa Cholakova's domain) being trained in Earth-observation methods (Dr. András Jung's domain) on a project that operates at their intersection.

The supervisory relationship with the fellow instantiates the two-way transfer of knowledge that WP4 structures formally. The host-to-fellow direction of the transfer is anchored in Dr. András Jung's group's competences: geoinformatics, remote sensing, hyperspectral imaging, field spectroscopy, and multisensor-drone-fusion methods and infrastructure. These are the competences that Dr. Rositsa Cholakova acquires through the fellowship and that she does not bring from her prior training as a plant physiologist. The fellow-to-host direction is anchored in Dr. Rositsa Cholakova's expertise in the non-destructive evaluation of the physiological status of drought-stressed tomato plants — expertise documented in her summa cum laude doctoral thesis (doi:10.2139/ssrn.5492527) and in her subsequent peer-reviewed work — which constitutes the biological and physiological ground-truth methodology that makes WP3 (Field validation, ground truth and uncertainty calibration) scientifically credible. This expertise is what the host's geoinformatics group does not independently possess: without the fellow's non-destructive plant-stress-phenotyping knowledge, the project could not specify, design, or execute the physiological measurement protocol in T3-01 (RQ4: chlorophyll fluorescence, stomatal conductance, canopy and leaf temperature, spectral reflectance). The exchange is therefore structurally non-trivial: neither party can execute this project without what the other brings.

Hosting arrangements

Dr. Rositsa Cholakova relocates from the Maritsa Vegetable Crops Research Institute, Bulgarian Agricultural Academy, Plovdiv, Bulgaria, to Eötvös Loránd University (ELTE), Budapest, Hungary, for the full 24-month duration of the fellowship. This Bulgaria-to-Hungary move is an intra-EU mobility satisfying the international mobility requirement under the MSCA-PF scope provisions (SR-01, SR-02). The fellowship is a confirmed European Fellowship of 24 months' duration.

The fellow is hosted for the full 24 months at the Faculty of Informatics, Institute of Cartography and Geoinformatics, under the supervision of Dr. András Jung. Eötvös Loránd University (ELTE), Budapest, as sole beneficiary and the legal entity that signs the grant agreement, provides all institutional support for the fellowship's duration: workplace and computing infrastructure commensurate with the project's geospatial and machine-learning computational requirements; HR support throughout the intra-EU relocation and for the duration of the fellowship period; access to the Faculty of Informatics academic community, seminars, colloquia, and research events; and full institutional compliance with all grant agreement obligations, including financial management, reporting to the European Commission, and institutional policy compliance. Matters requiring institutional escalation are handled through the Host Institutional Support Interface — Eötvös Loránd University (ELTE), Faculty of Informatics, Institute of Cartography and Geoinformatics — which, as the sole beneficiary legal entity, is the final institutional authority for all grant-level obligations.

No secondment and no non-academic placement are included in the fellowship plan. The fellowship operates as a full-duration, single-site 24-month European Fellowship at Eötvös Loránd University (ELTE), Budapest. This is a deliberate design choice, not a default or an omission. The project's scientific architecture — the diagnostic branch (WP1, months 1–14), the decision engine and demonstrator (WP2, months 6–20), and the field validation programme (WP3, months 8–24) — requires sustained, uninterrupted engagement with the host's geoinformatics infrastructure and with the supervisor's methodological expertise over the full duration. A secondment that removed the fellow from the host environment during, for example, the critical T1-03 calibration phase (months 8–12) or the T2-01 AquaCrop uncertainty-aware calibration phase (months 10–14) would interrupt the primary scientific dependency chain at its most sensitive juncture. The absence of a secondment maximises the depth of the host-to-fellow methods transfer and is consistent with the research-intensive, methodology-development nature of WP1 through WP3.

The fellow's integration into the host team is structured around two formal mechanisms that operate for the full 24 months. First, the Fellowship Management Committee — comprising Dr. Rositsa Cholakova and Dr. András Jung — meets monthly. The committee holds decision scope over strategic oversight of fellowship progress, review of milestone achievement against the work plan, approval of changes to the Career Development Plan, and resolution of issues beyond day-to-day research coordination. Monthly meetings are proportionate for a fellowship with five active work packages, an ongoing growing-season field campaign programme, and six milestones distributed across the 24 months. Second, WP4 (Training, supervision and two-way transfer of knowledge), led by Dr. András Jung and running from month 1 to month 24, structures the fellow's integration into the host group's scientific activities through regular research reviews, methods seminars, and a Career Development Plan (D4-01, due month 3) prepared jointly by supervisor and fellow and submitted as a project deliverable at the start of the action in compliance with SR-04. The Career Development Plan is reviewed and updated during the fellowship; the specific, measurable training and career targets it will contain — including publication planning, training targets, co-supervision, and career-progression milestones — are to be fixed jointly with the supervisor at the start of the action so that they reflect the actual state of the fellow's career at commencement, rather than being pre-asserted at proposal stage without that information.

T4-02 (Deliver structured transferable-skills training and supervision sessions, months 1–24) delivers the structured training dimension of WP4 throughout the full fellowship. This covers the MSCA-PF mandatory transferable-skills dimensions (SR-03): digital and AI skills (including responsible use of generative AI in remote-sensing and crop-water modelling workflows and scientific coding, directly grounded in the probabilistic modelling methods of WP1 and WP2); knowledge valorisation and innovation and entrepreneurship (translating the uncertainty-aware decision-support demonstrator toward user uptake, grounded in the demonstrator integration in WP2 and the exploitation logic in WP5, and mentored by the supervisor's track record with Cubert GmbH and Brightic Research); research integrity (grounded in the transparent uncertainty-calibration commitment that runs through every WP and in the open-science validation protocol); and open science and FAIR data stewardship (operationalised through D5-01, the Data Management Plan, due month 6, which establishes FAIR data stewardship for the multi-sensor dataset, calibration and validation datasets, and research demonstrator outputs).

All primary data sources are confirmed as available at the start of the fellowship, with no access negotiation required during the fellowship period. PlanetScope high-resolution imagery is secured through the Planet platform and API (RQ8, operator-confirmed before proposal submission). Sentinel-1 SAR and Sentinel-2 optical data are permanently available as open Copernicus data. ERA5 meteorological reanalysis is freely available from ECMWF. Hungarian tomato field sites are accessible through the associated partner AgroVIR. The operational consequence is that T1-01 (Acquire and harmonise multi-sensor EO and meteorological data, months 1–6) can begin data ingestion on day one of the fellowship. The multi-sensor data pipeline operational milestone (MS1, month 6) is achievable on schedule because the data infrastructure it depends on is pre-confirmed rather than contingent on in-fellowship negotiations.

Associated partner: AgroVIR

AgroVIR is the confirmed associated partner linked to the beneficiary Eötvös Loránd University (ELTE), Budapest. AgroVIR is based in Hungary. It does not receive EU funding from the grant; it is linked to the beneficiary as an associated partner providing real, scoped contributions to the project that extend what the beneficiary can accomplish alone.

AgroVIR's role is defined precisely: farmer access, field validation logistics, and feedback at Hungarian tomato field sites. It is not involved in research design, model development, or software engineering. It does not own or operate the uncertainty-aware decision-support tool. Its involvement does not extend to commercialisation or to operational deployment of the research demonstrator. This scoped role is exactly proportionate to what an associated partner contributes to a research fellowship targeting TRL 3–4 (RQ10), and it is the right scope: AgroVIR's contribution is to make real tomato farms accessible and to provide the farmer perspective that grounds the research artefact in real agricultural conditions, not to take responsibility for functions that belong to the research team.

Within WP3 (Field validation, ground truth and uncertainty calibration), AgroVIR's contribution is material and load-bearing:

In T3-01 (Design and run field validation protocol on Hungarian tomato farms, months 8–18), AgroVIR provides access to the Hungarian tomato farms at which Dr. Rositsa Cholakova runs the non-destructive physiological measurement campaigns. Without AgroVIR's farmer-access relationship, T3-01 could not be conducted at real tomato farms under real growing conditions. The non-destructive physiological ground-truth measurements specified in RQ4 — chlorophyll fluorescence, stomatal conductance, canopy and leaf temperature, and spectral reflectance — must be collected at real field sites growing real tomato during real growing seasons in order to produce the calibration and validation evidence that WP3 is designed to generate. AgroVIR makes this possible.

In T3-03 (Run co-located patch-covariation campaign for Route B validation, months 10–22), the same field-site access is required for the co-located seasonal campaign that tests Route B's central assumption: that the chosen homogeneous proxy patch genuinely covaries with the target tomato crop (RQ9). The campaign must measure the target crop and the proxy patch simultaneously at the same sites and across at least one full growing season. AgroVIR's access relationship is the enabling condition for this campaign.

At MS4 (Standalone research demonstrator exercises the end-to-end pipeline, month 18), AgroVIR facilitates farmer feedback on the research demonstrator web MVP. This feedback loop is how the tool's irrigation recommendations and uncertainty representations are assessed by the intended end-user community — Hungarian tomato growers — rather than only by the research team. At TRL 3–4, this form of early user feedback is the appropriate and productive level of engagement between the research artefact and the agricultural domain it targets.

AgroVIR's presence in the project additionally grounds the decision objective. Water-use efficiency (RQ6) is not an abstract modelling target but a metric with genuine agricultural significance in Hungarian row-crop horticulture. AgroVIR's role as a farmer-access partner embeds the decision engine's optimisation objective in the real context in which irrigation decisions are made and their consequences evaluated. This contextualisation is part of what makes OBJ-2 (Uncertainty-aware decision engine for water-use-efficient irrigation choice) a scientifically credible and practically meaningful objective rather than a modelling exercise disconnected from the conditions it claims to address.

The AgroVIR partnership does not require management overhead disproportionate to a single-researcher, single-beneficiary fellowship. The partner's contributions are field-logistics contributions — site access, measurement facilitation, farmer liaison — that AgroVIR delivers in parallel with WP3's measurement campaigns rather than requiring dedicated coordination structures beyond what the Fellowship Management Committee already handles. AgroVIR is involved through WP3 and WP5 activities; its involvement is continuous during the growing seasons covered by T3-01 and T3-03, and periodic for the MS4 demonstrator feedback. This is a lean and appropriate engagement model for an associated partner at the field-access stage of a research fellowship.
